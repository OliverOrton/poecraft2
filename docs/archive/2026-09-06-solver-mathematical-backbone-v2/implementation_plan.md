## 1. Outcome and scope

Oliver's primary objective is to extend **certified exact closure as far as practically achievable**. Fixed cohorts and budgets make comparisons interpretable; they do not cap ambition. Lower strength, upper quality, and preparation speed are intermediate outcomes, not substitutes for broader exact closure.

Deliver a readable mathematical explanation of the entire solver, prose claims with dependencies and falsifications, selected implementation links, a working research-chat-to-Codex handoff, cumulative interpretation of existing results, a small independent reference slice, and evidence-preserving cleanup. Claims are not machine-checked proofs. Existing numerical validators, scope checks, native mechanics, and publication authorities remain mandatory.

Keep the substantial measurement system: native benchmark, experiment identities, corpus roles, trajectories, Lab service/catalogue/CLI, native case validation, cancellation, reservations, matrix definitions and artifacts. Extend existing reporting; do not replace it with a new runner, database, dashboard or knowledge service.

Ordinary claim acceptance follows existing review with the actual responsible author/reviewer identified. Foundational semantic changes and ambiguous Path of Exile mechanics remain Oliver's decisions. An independent reference method is permitted as a development-only tool. Genuinely obsolete machinery may be removed after local consumer checks and preservation of its useful evidence. This does not authorize GUI deletion, solver tuning, new mechanics, default activation of retention, or the previous metamod feature plan.

## 4. Mathematical content: whole-solver coverage

Every major correctness boundary must lead to an actual argument or a precise open obligation, its dependencies, and its implementation consumer. Gaps must not be hidden behind “standard” or a test count. This release is not a claim to have formally verified all implementation code.

### 4.1 Target SSP

Define the versioned target `M_theta = (S,A,P,c,G,s0)` and intended policy class. State which item facts, observations, checkpoints and program/retry memory make it Markov. Preserve native goal overlap, tier/cardinality, rarity and junk-free semantics rather than assuming every bit is a distinct affix universally.

Explain complete caller action/program scope, generated families, dependency-only primitives, disabled/missing-price actions, goal-progress restrictions, voluntary Restart versus mechanic-owned recovery, and Imprint memory. Materialized rows are not the action set. Compute caps and bounded finish stop an algorithm, not silently redefine a finite-horizon task.

Define expected nonnegative accumulated cost, properness from a specified entry and finite-cost requirements. Recover the intended zero-cost/nontermination convention from current source and owner decisions. If they conflict, name the precise choice for Oliver; do not silently borrow PRISM semantics or change native behavior to fit a proof.

Separate native target, auxiliary optimistic model, fixed-policy graph, coefficient model and numerical implementation. A new solver implementation is a treatment, not necessarily a new target. A native semantic correction can change the target despite identical JSON. Use existing identities with a reviewed semantic alias/version, not another hashing service.

### 4.2 Representations

Explain physical items, coarse carriers, strict states, quotient cells and compiled nodes as distinct namespaces. State sufficient conditions for exact behavioral equivalence and distinguish them from optimistic relaxation. Describe exact identity/collision checking and full represented-member coverage.

Show why a successful representative lookup is not a uniform-class proof. A minimum of valid member-specific lowers can uniformly lower-bound a class; a maximum of member-specific proper policy costs can upper-bound all members when the required coverage and semantics hold. State the quantifiers and incomplete-domain refusal, not just min/max slogans. Preserve observation timing and all relevant checkpoint/program state.

### 4.3 Policies and upper bounds

Derive fixed-policy cost equations with the applicable properness/finite-cost assumptions. Explain cycles, retries, resource totals, complete exit probability, compiled routing and independent evaluation. Root properness does not prove arbitrary-entry coverage. Exact fixed-policy evaluation does not prove optimality.

For composed programs/options, show where mandatory steps, observation choices, setup/retry/cleanup cost and exits are represented. Do not skip a decision the caller may take. Explain candidate versus verified incumbent authority and what compilation/evaluation refusals mean.

### 4.4 Lowers and proof models

Give the Bellman subsolution argument under explicitly sufficient SSP assumptions, including boundary stopping and the boundedness/transversality assumptions actually used. Distinguish a checked finite subsolution from convergence to a greatest fixed point or a proof of infinity.

Explain complete canonical action/family coverage, scalar placeholders, exact inapplicability, optimistic exits and coupled regions. A missing action is not deferral. A stronger candidate vector must be checked against the appropriate full relation. An existing admissible lower need not be a feasible initialization for a differently truncated model.

Derive permitted composition: maximum of independently valid lowers, action-cost plus expected compatible continuation, and whole-state floors for all actions in the same complete scope. Addition needs a separate cost/decomposition argument. An action-restricted optimum cannot be promoted to a full-scope lower.

Recover current probability/retention arguments: conditional versus marginal probabilities; assignment/joint-event bounds; preserved/forced goals and overlaps; event-cap expectation minimization; value-specific minimizing distributions; rarity, capacity, destructive loss and crafted state; acquisition versus completion coordinates; applied rarity versus conditional refill occupancy; free exits imposing model ceilings. Preserve native projection and coefficient obligations separately from numerical feasibility.

### 4.5 Search, pruning and resumability

Separate correctness, eventual progress and efficiency. Explain why ordering may use noncertified scores while pruning needs a valid competitor lower and compatible executable evidence. State the actual tie/pruning contract. Do not label a sufficient scheduling restriction mathematically necessary without an argument.

Document incomplete envelopes, safe fallbacks, snapshot/row/generation compatibility, candidate capture/yield/resume/release and terminal refusal. A fixed candidate need not stay greedy to remain executable; structural validity and current quality differ. Name any fairness or finiteness premise needed for eventual completion and whether current code establishes it. Bounded-run failure is not a general completeness counterexample.

### 4.6 Numerical acceptance and exact closure

Distinguish native coefficients, stored binary equations, normalized references, directed arithmetic, residual evidence and exact arithmetic. Show a small residual-amplification counterexample. Explain why minimizing allocations must be rebuilt for changed values and why merely feasible allocations do not prove the minimum.

Finish with the conditional argument for a native exact result: compatible target/scope, complete authorized alternatives or valid retirement, proper executable policy, valid lower, correct identities and the declared numerical reconciliation. Map this to actual flags/statuses without claiming rounded zero gap or a status string alone proves exactness.

Use examples where they clarify an actual risk. Cite original theory and inspect its assumptions; label newly reconstructed arguments rather than inventing historical rationale. Mechanic ambiguities stay with Oliver. A discovered possible soundness defect is disclosed with its affected consumers; do not silently repair it under this documentation programme or rewrite the theorem to hide it.

## 5. Claim ledger and correspondence

Use stable IDs, for example `CLM-0001`, with prose fields: kind, exact statement, preconditions/model scope, canonical argument link, logical dependencies, evidence/implementation/test links, attempted falsifications, and append-only history with responsible reviewer and acceptance basis.

Kinds distinguish mathematics, native relations, empirical observations and conjectures. Statuses are `open`, `accepted`, `refuted`, `superseded`, `withdrawn`. Acceptance identifies its real basis; no invented independent review or conversion of finite tests into universal proof. An adequate conditional theorem may remain accepted when one native application fails its premises.

Material changes to a referenced statement or precondition get a new ID and explicit relation. Preserve the old proposition and challenge. Editorial link/typo fixes need no new claim; a brief change record distinguishes them from semantic alteration. Derive current status from history rather than maintaining a parallel handwritten table.

Separate logical dependence from motivation, test evidence and supersession. Check unjustified dependency cycles without confusing them with stochastic SCCs. Open premises remain visible at affected implementation boundaries; do not mark them accepted merely to make lint pass.

Seed the claims needed by the whole argument, roughly 15–25 as a planning guide rather than a quota. Include the recurring counterexamples and scope distinctions above. Do not create a claim for every helper, experiment or historical paragraph. Each mathematical boundary should show which premises the producer/checker establishes and which its caller must establish.

Source annotations support `uses`, `tests`, `refutes`, `obligation` and `historical`. An annotation is traceability, never runtime proof authority. No engine branch may read a documentation status to authorize a bound.

## 6. Research chats and Codex: a lightweight real round trip

Research sessions can inspect accessible sources and produce reports/code attachments without editing the local checkout. Codex can integrate local work. Neither role is assumed to see the other's live thread, terminal or unpushed changes. Direct repository access does not make a research argument automatically accepted.

**To the researcher:** provide the question/decision, available revision and scope, relevant claims and counterexamples, compatible reference results, and precise source links. One question-specific brief is enough. Do not send both a generic context dump and a duplicate export. No proof reading curriculum or full raw catalogue is required. Expand when the question needs it.

**From the researcher:** an existing report, attachment or pasted result is sufficient. Preserve substantive propositions, preconditions, actual derivations, evidence, counterexamples and unresolved premises. Distinguish inspected code, external theory, toy calculations, native measurements and proposed/unrun code. Provide usable citations beyond opaque chat IDs. A ZIP is optional; a new report rewrite is not required. Use temporary finding IDs only when they help track several findings.

**Codex intake:** identify the actual supplied report, inspect scripts before execution and extract archives safely. Preserve the useful original once, normally in the current boundary's `research-inputs/` and later its archive. Large raw outputs use existing artifact storage. Do not commit private full transcripts by default or treat an old Downloads filename as the requested source.

Check relevant differences from the reported baseline. Reuse existing claims when the argument is already known. Record each material finding as incorporated, open, refuted, duplicate, out of scope or blocked, with its canonical destination/reason. Group duplicates; a sentence with links can replace a table. Update only affected chapters/claims/questions/decisions. Preserve disagreements and the original report rather than silently editing its claims.

The usual final response carries the receipt: original report location, canonical argument/claim links, remaining issue, actual validation and real source/push status. No separate receipt file or intake-only session is mandatory. Missing optional evidence does not block unrelated work; state actual retrieval failures and request only genuinely necessary unavailable evidence.

**Return to research:** update affected generated views when their inputs change. A local sandbox path or chat link is not a durable repository reference. Until changes are pushed, transfer a compact explicit snapshot instead of implying that a read-only reviewer can see the local worktree. A new proposal must identify the changed premise when reopening a rejected approach.

Exercise this route once using the supplied backbone reports during programme qualification. Demonstrate real incorporation, reuse and a genuine open/disputed matter where the inputs support them; do not fabricate these outcomes. Subsequent routine fixes require no report, research question or intake ceremony.

## 11. Validation and recurrent operation

Run a focused check when it answers a real uncertainty or validates a retained change. No routine suite at each intermediate phase and no blanket ban on useful diagnostic tests. Documentation alone needs relevant links/lint/diff review, not a native build. New parser/report code needs focused fixtures. Source/build removal needs the smallest affected compile/test and inventory check. Inspect selectors to avoid accidental Simulator work.

Final acceptance is the affected coherent tooling/check set once, plus any actual downstream requirement of retained code changes. A full product pipeline is not the default. No strategy change is expected, so no Simulator is planned; do not silently change the owner-approved policy for genuine future strategy qualification. No large solver census or fresh clean-five run merely for knowledge/reporting acceptance. Unrun, skipped, failed and unavailable checks stay distinct.

After adoption, a small fix needs no new plan, claim, research packet or archive. Mathematical changes update their canonical argument/history; experiments update their relevant question/evidence; substantive imports receive one disposition. Do not mirror each update into every map and handoff. No per-command justification logs, read-count forms, automatic restamping, word-count CI or productivity dashboard.

Always-loaded instructions should stay roughly a page; task briefs should normally be short. These are editorial aims, not hard limits on necessary reasoning. Detailed proof content remains available and must be read when needed. If optional real session evidence is available, note a concrete avoided read/rewrite or caught missing premise; do not invent token/time savings from byte counts or require access to live threads to complete the programme.

On interruption, leave a coherent buildable checkpoint, one living record and HANDOFF naming the completed packages, current issue and exact next step. Do not claim full completion, archive unfinished production work as qualified, run broad suites because time is ending, promise background work, or push. The final response states actual commits/push status, arguments/gaps, mappings of imported findings, changes/removals/retained material, usable commands, actual reference evidence and checks, and one next research question.
