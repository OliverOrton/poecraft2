## 2. Why the previous capability hypothesis should stop

Two earlier handoff diagnostics did not discover a hidden cheaper native candidate: the 90-second selected snapshot checked at C5218.04, and the 120-second joint snapshot at C8473.16. Those results refute the practical justification for repeating these treatments. They do not establish that every possible source-score screen is economically perfect. The distinction matters: retain the general mathematical warning about using an upper estimate as a rejection proof, but stop treating it as the demonstrated current cause.

This review's earlier framing should have controlled the known native-versus-worker call policy before selecting more seed changes. The new plan corrects that order. It does not relabel a new seed preference as a scheduling fix.

## 4. Current CI: a small but real new failure

The knowledge workflow succeeds. Windows successfully builds and prepares the frozen runtime but stops in the first Python test family: **162 pass, five skip, one fails**. The failing test measures the whole `SolverLabSupervisor.run_once()` around a real child requested to time out at 0.15 seconds. Its observed elapsed time is 1.8909999999999627 seconds against a 1.5-second ceiling. Forwarding of the requested watchdog passes. CTest and later web layers were not reached by this hosted attempt. This is not a reappearance of the old native interval failures. [R9,R10]

The fixture hardcodes its returned `status` to `watchdog_expired`; it does not assert the raw worker's `timed_out` and `survivor` fields before the timing assertion. Later resource assertions are unreachable after that failure. The existing subprocess owner already records those fields, process identity and elapsed time. Preserve and check them. Timing the whole supervisor also includes admission, metadata, spawn, kill/reap and persistence beyond the requested child deadline. The hosted log does not identify which interval caused the overrun. [R10,R11]

Resolve this by a targeted fixture/owner diagnosis, not by blindly raising the bound, adding retries, skipping the test, or replacing the actual child's result with an expected string. Python's documentation explicitly notes that process creation can delay timeout observation, but that general fact does not diagnose this failure. [P3]

## 6. External research and what it does not authorize

The 2026 CG-iLAO* paper by Johannes Schmalz and Felipe Trevizan is directly relevant to avoiding unnecessary action computations while preserving optimality obligations. Its finite SSP and cost/properness premises are explicit. Poecraft2 already has delayed actions and separate proof authority; importing another planner is not the selected task. The useful lesson is to measure which action computations are repeated or omitted and why, rather than equating raw row volume with progress. [P1]

V8's documented compilation tiers and cold/warm distinctions require recording actual Node/V8/WASM identities and using normal release timing for acceptance. Profilers can alter compilation behaviour. The earlier first-policy investigation already rejected first-use compilation as its sole explanation. Nothing here reopens that rejected diagnosis without interval-specific evidence. [P2]
