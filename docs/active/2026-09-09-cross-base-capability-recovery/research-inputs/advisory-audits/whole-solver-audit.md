# Whole-solver capability and falsifiable abstractions

## Verdict and three recommendations

**The strongest next computational change is to ask a threshold question about a live proof obligation, rather than automatically constructing enough continuation and partition state to solve it exactly.** Use the existing lower-only machinery to obtain either a sufficient source/action lower certificate or a cheap, independently checked witness that the current optimistic model cannot reach the required threshold. Do not build another general planner, basin library, or lower solver first.

The evidence does **not** support a universal “the solver needs a larger heuristic” diagnosis. At the pinned snapshot, Conquest four-goal proof actually consumes stronger local lowers but remains occupied by frontier completion; five-goal proof stops at partition memory. Historical Ring/Amulet and weapon records expose different earlier barriers. Candidate quality and retention of compatible historical policies remain competing explanations, not solved prerequisites.[^6][^8][^10]

1. **Test one threshold-directed, open-frontier proof question using existing owners.** Require root-relevant headroom before extending its model. This is the preferred research direction, not an instruction to interrupt the cross-base programme.
2. **Keep the no-new-abstraction alternative competitive.** Reconcile the partial-three-to-five historical policy reference and inspect sharing of existing partition payloads before commissioning a larger representation.
3. **Reserve query-specific stochastic integration for a demonstrated row-generation bottleneck.** It must skip native probability or successor work, not merely store the same dense computation differently.

## 1. End-to-end capability diagnosis

### 1.1 Scope and terminal semantics come before optimization

The objective is minimum expected cost under the declared native request and proper-policy semantics. The current goal contract includes the required goal coverage, rarity, and the junk-free terminal condition; a partially completed or dirtier item is not automatically success. The distinction between raw accumulated cost and proper termination remains explicit in the mathematical contract. The quality-ladder product configurations also restrict control: generated Imprint and voluntary economic Restart are off, with goal-progress-gated reforges. These restrictions belong to the measured policy class, not to unrestricted crafting optimality.[^1][^2][^8]

Consequently, two superficially similar five-mod requests can have different feasible controllers and values. A smaller junk-free subgoal is especially dangerous: terminality for that subgoal need not include terminality for the original final goal. It may guide proposals, but terminating there cannot supply the original continuation for free. Similarly, a vocabulary frozen for diagnostic handoff leaves original discovery obligations open.[^2][^3][^6]

**Consequence and counterargument.** Some apparent “missing clever strategy” cases can be scope or terminal-policy restrictions rather than implementation defects. Conversely, declared restrictions do not excuse missing actions within that scope. **Smallest check:** compare canonical target, action/program, control, data, and economy identities before comparing costs. **Urgency:** high for interpreting every result; no mechanic change is proposed.

### 1.2 Representation and successor generation already exploit substantial structure

`solver_reforge.cpp` does not enumerate literal independent modifier draws naively. Its native recurrence groups goal/below-tier and compatible junk families, records availability/exclusion effects, and carries enough state to update remaining weights. The denominator couples the sides through their remaining eligible weight. Exact support remains potentially large despite this compression.[^4]

The older complete-frontier census found almost full Cartesian products of one-sided payloads, not a neglected pocket of duplicate states. This limits support-only merging; it does **not** prove every expectation query requires materializing that support. It also explains why preserving only goal bits or assuming independent sides is not a sound universal replacement.[^10][^11]

There is an important supersession: final-depth V3 factorization was initially rejected, then integrated for strict rows after evaluator-neutral work accounting corrected the comparison. Its binding-row median improved 27.25%, but the recorded aggregate solve improvement was only 0.30%; the selected closure still did not reach a partition in the larger diagnostic run. Coarse rows remained V1. Recommending V3 as absent, or calling its original rejection permanent, would both be wrong.[^12][^13]

**Consequence and counterargument.** New representation work must reduce the computation a consumer needs, not rename an existing terminal accumulator. A cheap layout improvement can still help even without reducing logical support. **Smallest check:** attribute one binding obligation to conditional probability propagation, successor interning, selected closure, or replay—not a single undifferentiated “reforge work” count. **Urgency:** high on broad-pool cases; the best reduction depends on the owner.

### 1.3 Admission, scheduling, and continuation assembly are not missing wholesale

The capability chain already includes delayed/generated action accounting, ordinary joint-policy search, retained candidate-local continuations, properness checking, compilation, and independent fixed-policy evaluation. The September 9 branch adds benchmark-only pre-finish candidate handoff, demanded native rows beyond saved policy ownership, deferred option-kernel construction, and prepared lower consumption in strict alternatives. These are present mechanisms, not recommendations to implement from scratch.[^3][^5][^6][^7][^31]

Their effects differ. The earlier B1/B2 comparison produced the same large upper improvement with lower consumption disabled: preparation timing changed the trajectory. Later bounded sibling-continuation work did improve the verified empty-five upper from approximately 17.0 million to 85,558.7062 without changing its lower. That is useful candidate assembly, not exact proof. Eager candidate resumption also had a rejected starvation-prone variant; “service this candidate sooner” is not a universal scheduling improvement.[^6]

The ordinary/diagnostic boundary matters. F7/F8 describe the optional long proof treatment. The final ordinary short runs retain four-goal and five-goal verified policies without inheriting those long treatments' exactness claims or memory classification. The fixed saved controller remains immutable; a newly explored continuation is a new candidate until the native publication path verifies it.[^6][^7]

**Consequence and counterargument.** Candidate quality can dominate a sophisticated lower improvement. However, the current record no longer supports the simple diagnosis “strict proof never receives time” or “prepared lowers have no consumer.” **Smallest check:** identify whether the blocked object is a candidate continuation, an all-action lower obligation, or a fixed-controller evaluation. **Urgency:** immediate for directing work, not justification for another scheduling sweep.

## 3. Ranked directions and two mathematical finalists

### Direction A — Threshold-directed finite proof, with an impossibility witness for the current relaxation

**Rank 1; near-term extension of existing owners.** Represent a finite set of exact semantic entries, or independently justified lower classes, plus independent boundary lowers. Forget the need to route the incumbent outside that set; do not forget legal alternatives, observation state, or positive-mass exits.

For every interior state/action, check

\[
x(s)\le c(s,a)+\sum_{t\in R}P(t\mid s,a)x(t)
                 +\sum_{t\notin R}P(t\mid s,a)b(t). \tag{1}
\]

Here `b` independently lower-bounds the same native final-goal problem. Complete scalar/family lower constraints may cover actions not expanded. This is the existing finite-region principle in **CLM-0012**, not a new theorem or a missing lower solver. The September 4 free-value report already proposed this mathematical separation.[^15][^22][^30]

**The additional formulation is a threshold decision with two kinds of evidence.** For first-action retirement, force that action at the query entry and set a threshold above the compatible source upper. A checked feasible potential reaching the threshold is positive evidence. A proper policy in the *optimistic model* costing less than the threshold is negative evidence: the current model cannot prove enough, irrespective of how well its numerical solver is optimized. That policy is an auxiliary ceiling, not an executable native upper.

This reverses an unproductive pattern: do not first maximize a weak auxiliary model and then discover it never had enough headroom. September C0 already supplies a native motivation. Its approximately 405.36940215 optimistic Scour/Alchemy/Fracture route leaves almost no improvement over the checked five-goal lower; tightening only Chaos cannot remove that surviving route. The conclusion is specific to the unchanged model and surviving witness, not all future retention abstractions.[^6]

**Producer → consumer.** `ProductionPolicyOracle`/the persistent alternative scheduler identifies the source/action question; existing prepared lowers and complete available rows supply a bounded lower-only view; `QuotientBellmanGraph::check_lower`/`solve_lower` checks it; the existing alternative ledger consumes only a compatible first-action certificate. Proposed work is the pre-partition producer/consumption connection, not a second checker or a second full graph. Exact singleton identities are the conservative fallback where no class-wide lemma exists. The current implementation has not been shown to support this entire bridge; its local upper-improvement owner still requires compatible successor policy values.[^17][^22][^23][^32]

**Opportunity and falsifier.** Appendix A gives a cyclic/destructive SSP whose root optimum 10 can be certified without an incumbent route from a deviation state. A weaker boundary makes the optimistic optimum only 5; the ceiling witness then rejects further numerical work. Omitting one legal action creates a false certificate immediately.

**Cost/generalization.** The patch retains its own identities, rows, boundary certificates, and reverse dependencies, all within existing total limits. It can bypass a continuation/closed-partition extension only when the boundary is strong enough. Armour and Bow both possess retained useful-policy evidence and may benefit from such separation; this is a structural rationale, not current measured coverage. Ring/Amulet are hostile when their cheap optimistic escape or huge row arises before any useful boundary. The main risk is weak evidence, not failure of the first-exit theorem.

### Direction B — Integrate a frozen value query at a stopping line of the native draw process

**Rank 3 for implementation; materially different representation research.** Instead of compressing completed physical states into a universal quotient, represent partial native draw histories only until the requested expectation can be bounded. A history must retain every field determining its conditional law: preserved base, available/excluded families, occupancy, guaranteed-draw phase, and any permitted decision information.[^4]

For an exhaustive, disjoint stopping line `C` through a finite action's draw tree, let `w(h)` be its exact probability mass. If a native-uniform certificate establishes `ell(h) ≤ E[v(Y) | h]`, then

\[
c+\sum_{h\in C}w(h)\ell(h)\le c+\mathbb E[v(Y)]. \tag{2}
\]

This may give a lower expectation; equality can hold for one frozen `v` without yielding equality for other continuations or all policies. All mass—including failed progress and nonterminal exits—remains present. Appendix B demonstrates two cut contributions replacing a sum over 256 synthetic labels, and failures caused by hidden context, changed values, or lost observation timing.

**Producer → consumer.** A proposed query interface beside the existing `CalcContext` reforge recurrence could emit a typed expectation certificate into Direction A or the existing lower model. Its initial domain is a finite primitive draw kernel; cyclic programs require a separately proved complete first-exit law. It must not install an unfinished query as an `OutcomeDistribution`, exact policy kernel, or universal class row. It is not proposed as a fixed-policy upper shortcut. Existing event-envelope support can be reused only with its real domain; value-specific minimizers must be recomputed or re-certified.[^4][^15][^22]

**Why this differs from old attempts.** A successful cut skips suffix propagation and successor interning. The rejected DAG replayed action-specific edges; terminal V3 still needed denominator/subset work. Dense side support does not forbid a simple frozen value function, but neither does a simple value function guarantee a cheap conditional expectation. A candidate must exhibit a native stopping condition that removes operations before completion, not merely a smaller output vector.

**Cost/generalization.** Let `E` be native edge updates, `Ecut` those before the cut, `K` compatible queries, and `B+C` setup/checking work. A necessary work-benefit condition is `K(E−Ecut) > B+C`, including rechecks after value changes. Memory is the retained prefix frontier plus certificates, not automatically proportional to the small output. A favorable non-Conquest regime would be an Amulet or Ring row with many junk completions sharing a proved continuation floor; the retained dense supports show the possible output saving, not the required uniformity. The same mechanism could serve a weapon retry/recovery potential when hidden identities are provably irrelevant to that query. Rare mixed-goal weapons with identity-sensitive best continuations, and densely coupled Amulet prefixes, can defeat it. No favorable native stopping line has yet been established.

### Direction C — No new abstraction: compatible-policy retention and payload sharing

**Rank 2, and the first choice if its small check succeeds.** Use the existing portfolio/evaluator to reconcile the historical partial policy; use existing replay ownership to share immutable data. A better verified upper lowers the burden of every compatible alternative comparison. A shared partition payload can avert a peak without weakening native authority. Neither requires a persistent endgame library.[^7][^8][^14][^17]

Deterministic feature ordering is the cheaper baseline for a scheduling hypothesis. Learned ranking can propose the same priorities, but cannot confer bound, transition, equivalence, or pruning authority. Native parallelism can reduce serial wall work where independent jobs exist; it does not itself remove state/partition obligations and may increase simultaneous memory. No inspected matched result establishes that either dominates these changes. These are competing hypotheses, not prescribed additions.

## 4. Primary literature: what transfers and what does not

**Chatterjee et al., TACAS 2025, “Fixed Point Certificates for Reachability and Expected Rewards in MDPs.”** Section 5.1/Lemma 6 treats lower certificates with explicit finiteness and nonreachability semantics; a lower need not be an incumbent value. This supports Direction A's certificate viewpoint, not its native abstraction or action coverage. The local finite-region argument is already present in the repository.[^24]

**Schmalz and Trevizan, Artificial Intelligence 2026, “Efficient Constraint Generation for Stochastic Shortest Path Problems.”** The journal treatment extends the AAAI 2024 work. It supports selective action-constraint work and careful handling of consistency. Its finite SSP/positive-cost and termination/error assumptions cannot silently replace the engine's contracts; its reduction in Q computations does not establish cheaper procedural native rows. The adaptation here is threshold-first stopping with a checked auxiliary ceiling, not importing reported speedups.[^25]

**Watanabe et al., CAV 2024, “Compositional Value Iteration with Pareto Caching.”** It reuses component analysis across boundary-weight queries in compositional reachability MDPs. This motivates query-oriented interfaces rather than materializing every possible controller. It assumes a supplied compositional structure and does not discover a small native separator. Its reachability method is not automatically a proper-cost solver, and sound stopping does not establish general finite termination.[^26]

**Holtzen, Van den Broeck, and Millstein, OOPSLA 2020, “Scaling Exact Inference for Discrete Probabilistic Programs.”** Dice compiles bounded/nonrecursive discrete programs to weighted model counting and exploits conditional or local structure. This supports investigating query-specific elimination without assuming independent sides. It does not guarantee compact compilation of the native exclusion recurrence or value function. No Dice dependency or whole-engine symbolic rewrite is recommended.[^27]

The established ingredients are subsolution certificates, first-exit reasoning, constraint generation, and structured expectation computation. Their proposed placement before the current strict closure/replay work is an engineering adaptation. **No novelty claim is made**, including for the threshold-ceiling combination. A research contribution would need an explicit native correspondence theorem and a demonstrated reduction in root-relevant computation beyond the retained implementations and published methods.

Fixed-controller SCC elimination is also legitimate but not the preferred separate project. For a transient internal block, its reward/exit operator is `(I−P_RR)^{-1}c` and `(I−P_RR)^{-1}P_RB`. Large boundaries, changed internal choices, and arbitrary-entry routing can erase the savings. Two locally terminating options can still compose into a nonterminating cycle. This is already constrained by CLM-0004; another scalar basin summary would not answer it.[^14][^15][^16]

Use the already selected bounded-finish profile and no new parameter sweep. Reuse available rows and prepared bounds first. A new strict checkpoint is not assumed: supported development replay skips completed coarse transitions, not current strict partition state.[^7]

Report preparation, discovery, row construction, proof, partition, compilation, evaluation, serialization, and cleanup time; report both native-owned peak and process memory with their attribution. Keep setup outside a measured subphase from becoming “free.” From F7 alone, eliminating all 178.287 s of strict work could remove at most 74.07% of its 240.690 s total **with the same remaining work**; this is a ceiling, not a prediction or a matched speedup.[^6]

Local floors, row counts, and auxiliary optima are separate observations—not successful root outcomes. A single pair can discriminate a mechanism but cannot establish a statistical speedup.

Only three missing evidence items could materially change the recommendation: matched current non-Conquest outcomes; identity and present evaluation of the historical partial controller; and allocation/obligation detail at the actual next frontier stop. None requires reopening the entire archive or pausing the current programme.

## Appendix A. Finite-region proof, threshold duality, and counterexamples

### A.1 Represented object and guarantee

Let the original SSP minimize nonnegative expected cost among allowed policies reaching the true goal almost surely, with the nontermination convention explicit. Let `R` be finite. An entry includes the physical item and every control/checkpoint/offer distinction necessary for legal actions and their conditional transitions. Lower-only abstraction is permitted only with a proved native-covering relation; an exact representative is not a proof for all members.

Choose a bounded nonnegative potential `x` on `R`, zero on true goals. At outside entries choose independent, same-scope, finite/integrable lowers `b(t) ≤ V*(t)`. Cover every native action by equation (1), a sound complete family/scalar floor, or a complete inapplicability proof. Values inside `R` may be solved simultaneously through cycles; outside bounds cannot be provisional circular promises.

Fix any proper native policy. Stop at the first true goal, first exit from `R`, or first scalar-covered action. Repeated conditional application of the interior inequalities gives, at truncated stopping time `T∧n`, a bound by accumulated costs plus the stopped payoff. The outside payoff is no greater than the policy's remaining expected cost; a scalar-covered action is bounded by that action plus its complete continuation. On paths still inside, the remaining potential is bounded by `max_R x`, while properness makes the probability of that tail tend to zero. Nonnegative accumulated costs admit monotone convergence. Thus `x(s)` is below every proper policy cost, hence below `V*(s)`.

This proves a **lower**, not a controller, universal quotient, convergence rate, or feasibility of a particular numerical iterate. It is the CLM-0007/0008/0012 argument specialized to the proposed consumer.[^15]

For a first-action query, introduce an entry with only the chosen first action; thereafter preserve all original actions. A certified lower at that entry is a lower on `Q*_a(s)`. Retire it only against an upper certified for the **same semantic source**, using the existing safe comparison/tie contract. A root upper cannot stand in for a partial-entry upper.

### A.2 A synthetic cyclic/destructive example

There are three nonterminal states and one goal `g`:

| State/action | Cost | Complete transition law |
|---|---:|---|
| `r / finish` | 10 | `g` with probability 1 |
| `r / deviation` | 2 | `g` with probability 1/2; `q` with probability 1/2 |
| `q / retry` | 1 | `q` with probability 1/2; destructive exit `d` with probability 1/2 |
| `d / rebuild` | 40 | `g` with probability 1 |

The retry is transient: `V(q)=1+V(q)/2+40/2=42`. Therefore the deviation costs 23 and the root optimum is 10. A root controller that contains only `finish` need not have a route from `q`. The lower proof on `R={r,q}` with independent boundary `b(d)=40` nevertheless certifies the comparison. All destructive exit mass is charged.

Now weaken only the boundary to `b(d)=4`. The optimistic model has `x(q)=6` and root deviation cost 5. Its proper deviation/retry/boundary policy is a finite **upper on that auxiliary model**, demonstrating that no feasible subsolution of this model can establish root lower 10. Better numerical solving cannot cross that ceiling. In general its forced-deviation value is `3+b(d)/2`; a tie at 10 needs `b(d)=14`, and strict separation needs `b(d)>14`.

Remove the complete-action assumption by overlooking a legal `q→g` action costing 1. The true root optimum becomes `2+1/2=5/2`, while the old alleged certificate still says 10. Alternatively, a legal completion costing 8 makes the true root optimum 6: that is evidence to improve the candidate, not to explain away a failed inequality.

### A.3 Why the auxiliary ceiling is a stopping certificate

Let `M_R` be the complete optimistic model embodied by the proposed constraints, including its explicit boundary/scalar stopping payments. For any proper finite-cost policy `μ` in that model, the same telescoping argument gives

\[
x(r)\le J^{\mu}_{M_R}(r)
\]

for every feasible subsolution `x`. Therefore `J^{μ}_{M_R}(r)<τ` disproves attainability of `x(r)≥τ` **in this model**. It does not disprove the stronger native lower or optimality of the incumbent. It also says nothing about a refined model in which the witness's transitions, permissible decisions, or boundary payments changed.

The positive certificate and negative ceiling need not come from a full LP solve. The same retained sparse machinery can check a supplied potential or a supplied auxiliary fixed policy. The new consumer is a proposal; the mathematical relation is standard certificate duality, not a novelty claim.

### A.4 Identity, invalidation, and numerical obligations

Bind the query to native target/control scope, data/pool, prices, coefficient interpretation, exact source or whole member domain, model revision, boundary certificates, and action coverage. Newly generated actions invalidate a prior completeness assertion unless an existing family certificate already quantified over them. Split classes must invalidate affected source/target evidence. Changed boundary models need not preserve feasibility of old values; keep an independently valid old native lower separately at compatible maximum consumption rather than forcing it into the changed model.[^7][^15]

Use the retained conservative arithmetic/checker, not an arbitrary residual tolerance. Its exact dyadic checks certify statements about the supplied coefficient representation; a native stochastic relation still needs its own coefficient/projection proof. Long retry horizons can amplify a small Bellman residual. These illustrations use exact rationals and establish no native floating-point qualification.[^23]

Finally, two options `r→q` and `q→r` each terminate locally but compose into a closed non-goal cycle. Local finite first exit is sufficient to summarize an internal reward/exit law, not to publish the global controller. A genuine trap exit cannot be discarded or normalized into success.

## Appendix B. Query-specific integration and attempted falsification

### B.1 Stopping-line object

Within a finite native stochastic action, let `H` be its execution-history tree. A stopping line contains exactly one prefix on every positive-probability completed path. Let `Y` be the complete successor, including any control information relevant to continuation. By conditioning,

\[
\mathbb E[v(Y)]=\sum_{h\in C}w(h)\mathbb E[v(Y)\mid h].
\]

Substituting certified conditional lowers gives equation (2). A merged history key must provide the bound for **every** represented history, unless its authority is explicitly limited to one producing distribution. An average under that distribution cannot become an arbitrary-entry value.

For equality on one frozen query, the omitted information must not change the conditional expected `v`. For a universal behavioral quotient it must preserve costs, action/observation structure, and class transitions for every relevant continuation. The first is strictly weaker. This is why a query cut need not repeat the failed state-merging premise.[^11][^15]

### B.2 Opportunity with full mass and a retry cycle

At synthetic partial state `r`, a direct finish costs `13/2`. A broad action costs 1, reaches goal with probability `1/4`, and with probability `3/4` destroys progress and generates one of 256 distinct junk labels via eight fair binary draws. Each junk state has a repair action costing 4, reaching goal with probability `1/2` and returning to that same junk state otherwise. It also has a label-visible terminal action costing 100 or 101.

Repair is proper and costs 8. The value 8 satisfies every junk-state action inequality. Label-dependent action costs prevent simply identifying all states under unchanged full action-wise equality, even though this particular continuation potential is constant. Full enumeration and the two-prefix cut both give

\[
Q=1+\tfrac14\cdot0+\tfrac34\cdot8=7>13/2.
\]

If the uniform continuation fact is already available, the query can stop before generating the labels. It retains the entire failure mass and its cyclic recovery cost. This illustrates removable work; proving the analogous uniform native fact is still the missing bridge.

### B.3 Failures the interface must refuse or refine

**Changed potential.** Set the queried potential of one junk label to zero while leaving the other 255 at eight. The new expectation is `893/128`, not 7. Reusing 7 as the exact or lower value of the *new query* is invalid. An older independently valid native lower may still survive separately if its native semantics remain unchanged; it must not be relabelled as the changed model's Bellman evidence.

**Hidden conditional law.** Suppose an omitted history bit changes goal probability to `1/2` or `1/4`, with failure continuation eight. The two action values are five and seven. Their equally weighted producing-row average is six. Six is not a uniform lower on the first entry; seven is not even the mixture value. Retaining the context, proving a uniform minimum of five, or refusing are sound alternatives. This is the same kind of lost denominator/context premise that defeats simple side factorization.

**Observed decisions.** Two equally likely offers have costs `(0,8)` and `(8,0)`. Permitted post-observation choice costs zero; committing beforehand costs four. Moving the minimum outside the expectation would overstate a lower for the permitted controller. A frozen policy query must instead use that policy's actual decision map.

**Missing exits or stale minimizers.** A cut with 0.9 represented mass cannot normalize that mass to one and erase a 0.1 nonterminal/trap exit. Likewise, value-independent probability caps do not make their minimizing allocation value-independent: caps `(3/5,3/5)` and a swap from values `(0,10)` to `(10,0)` have minimum four, while replaying the old allocation yields six. Complete mass and current-vector minimization remain required.[^15]

### B.4 Native integration and cost limits

The first implementation boundary, if selected, is one lower-only query over an existing recurrence—not a symbolic copy of the whole solver. Cache identity includes source/control domain, action and forced-draw semantics, native pool/exclusion relation, stopping-line proof, potential/boundary dependency version, and arithmetic interpretation. Price-independent structure can survive a price change only when it truly is independent; the priced value certificate must be checked again.[^4][^7]

Native rows with history-dependent remaining weights may force the query to retain nearly the full prefix frontier. For the historical Amulet, avoiding 3,204,323 completed successors does not demonstrate avoidance of the 2,328,144-state peak roll frontier or all 151,348,836 reported work units. The implementation must count actual skipped recurrence/denominator work and peak live payload, with setup and final checking included.[^10]

The companion script performs **19 exact rational checks** covering the two examples and their failures. It uses an independent small stationary-policy enumeration for Appendix A and direct full-sum versus cut arithmetic for Appendix B. It neither proves the general theorems nor validates their native correspondence.
