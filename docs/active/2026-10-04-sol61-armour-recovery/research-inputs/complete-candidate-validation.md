## 6. Mathematical and ownership arguments

These are applications of existing fixed-policy, resource-accounting and lifetime arguments. They are not claims of a new solver theorem or a new mathematical contribution. Native correspondence is stated separately from the conditional derivations. [M01] [M02] [M03]

### 6.1 Fixed-policy value and qualified feasible upper

**Definitions.** Expand a fixed compiled controller into the finite product of exact item state, graph/control location and all relevant checkpoint, offer, observed-choice and mandatory-programme state. Let `Q` be its nonterminal transition matrix, `r` its vector of complete expected immediate costs, and `α` the original-root distribution. All permitted terminal categories must be represented; failure or unresolved mass is not renormalized into success.

**Proposition.** If the original-root reachable nonterminal controller is transient, every transition and paid resource is native-correct and in scope, and all mandatory entry-admission obligations have been discharged, then

\[
N=(I-Q)^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Q^k,
\qquad J_\pi=Nr,
\qquad J_\pi(\alpha)=\alpha^TNr.
\]

If this controller is feasible for semantic request `I`, its value upper-bounds the optimal proper-policy value for that request:

\[
V_I^*(\alpha)\le J_\pi(\alpha).
\]

**Proof.** Transience gives convergence of the Neumann series. The kth term accounts for expected cost incurred after k nonterminal transitions; summing gives the Bellman solution. The feasible fixed controller belongs to the policy class over which the optimum is minimized, so its value cannot be below that optimum. Native entry admission is part of the premise that the purported controller actually belongs to that class. The matrix calculation alone does not prove native permission for a dependency-only programme occurrence. This is the existing CLM-0002 argument, with the native compiler/admission correspondence kept explicit. [M01] [C06]

**Numerical qualification.** Native graph-based evaluation uses its existing floating-point convergence, properness, mass, residual and reconciliation contract. This packet neither replaces that contract with exact rational arithmetic nor calls the reported decimal an outward-rounded rigorous enclosure. If `v` is approximate and `e = r + Qv - v`, then

\[
J_\pi-v=(I-Q)^{-1}e.
\]

A small residual alone is not a dimension-free error bound: a nearly recurrent chain can have a large fundamental matrix. Numerical acceptance must remain exactly the repository's established contract, not a looser threshold introduced to obtain the desired 210k acceptance. [M02]

**Scope counterexample.** A root can go directly to the goal while an unrelated graph entry loops forever. Root properness remains true, but a continuation upper for that unrelated entry is false. The present census is root-reachable, yet each positive native programme occurrence still needs the separate legality/resource/admission proof; root success does not issue that proof automatically.

### 6.2 Why every positive entry must remain

Let `E+` be the finite set of reached programme entries with positive root occupancy. A complete entry certificate requires

\[
\forall e\in E^+:\quad
\text{exact binding}(e)\ \land\ \text{native programme admitted}(e)
\ \land\ \text{complete paid outcomes}(e).
\]

Caching may reduce the cost of proving a conjunct; it cannot remove the conjunct. A completed index or bit per entry records that obligation's result without changing its probability mass. Where the current implementation uses an ordered cursor, retaining that cursor is sufficient; a new status database is unnecessary. [S01] [S05]

**Falsification by a small-probability tail.** Consider a synthetic controller which finishes normally with probability `1−ε` and reaches an expensive tail with probability `ε>0`. Give that tail cost `1/ε²`. Omitting it loses `1/ε` expected cost, however small ε is. Replacing the tail by a non-goal recurrent class destroys almost-sure success. Therefore a numerical probability tolerance cannot justify dropping the entry. This is a synthetic counterexample, not a PoE mechanic or a measured native failure.

**Mandatory programme composition.** If a programme has complete exit distribution `p_e(y)` and expected internal paid cost `k_e`, then its cost with compatible continuation `J` is

\[
k_e+\sum_y p_e(y)J(y).
\]

The formula requires full exit mass, actual information timing, finite exit behavior, and inclusion of every acquisition/setup/cleanup/recovery operation belonging to the programme. Even if each local programme exits almost surely, alternating two such programmes forever can create a globally improper controller. The complete compiled controller still needs its ordinary root evaluation. This preserves CLM-0004's conditional argument and its open general native correspondence. [M01] [C05]

### 6.3 Cumulative work invariant

Let each executed native work event have nonnegative integer charge `w_j`. Let `D_n` be cumulative committed logical work. With a fixed cap `W`, a correct shared owner maintains

\[
D_0=0,\qquad D_{n+1}=D_n+w_{n+1},\qquad
w_{n+1}\le W-D_n
\]

before that event executes. A parent may already have committed work when the validation child is created; that starting debit is retained rather than reset.

**Derivation.** Induction gives `D_n≤W`. A child forwards through the complete owner chain before execution, so the smallest remaining allowance is respected. A cooperative suspension introduces no new event and leaves `D` unchanged. Cancellation may reclaim uncommitted storage but cannot undo already executed work. Hence rollback of staged publication never refunds `D`.

For a checker which reports cumulative counters rather than directly sharing the parent, maintain a monotone watermark `C_seen`; charge `C_now−C_seen` exactly once, then advance the watermark after successful debit. Enforce the checker's genuinely remaining allowance during execution, not merely after it finishes. The benchmark's generation/checker/entry decomposition must reconcile with the parent sum on every terminal path. [S03] [S07] [S08]

**Concrete falsification already obtained.** Reporting only 40 ordinary work while 48 automatic units also execute produces 88 actual units against cap 50. Clamping a later report to the remaining allowance cannot retrospectively prevent those events. This is the independent Finder defect, not a theory-only concern. Conversely, the private six-entry validator's 1/47/48 threshold controls and retained shared debit directly exercise the corrected owner path. [E11] [E13]

### 6.4 Live allocation ledger and aggregate envelope

Define a selected allocation universe for the native ownership contract. Partition its live allocations at time `t` among disjoint accounting owners `A_i(t)`. Shared aliases point to one allocation; separately allocated equal-value copies are different allocations. Let `S(t)` represent transient scratch/reallocation storage not already contained in those owners. Then

\[
M(t)=\sum_i \operatorname{bytes}(A_i(t))+S(t).
\]

“Selected allocation” is deliberate: this is not an identity with resident set size, allocator-retained pages, browser heap size or total operating-system process memory. Those require separate observations and definitions. [M03]

**Conditional ledger argument.** Suppose initialization accounts for all selected owned capacities; every selected allocation/growth/release updates the appropriate owner or is covered by an active reservation; shared storage is charged once; and activation/deactivation transfers charges without loss. Then the maintained live ledger is conservative for selected live ownership. This follows by induction over allocation and ownership-transfer events. Replacing a full traversal with that ledger changes observation cost, not the accounted semantic object.

**Native evidence and proof gap.** The finite full-versus-ledger audits and the active-child implementation support this correspondence for the inspected paths. They do not by themselves enumerate every allocation site, every platform's capacity policy or every transient interval. The root receipt's incomplete-aggregate flag is therefore correct. A one-time whole-calculator scan cannot establish coverage of all subsequent mutable scratch. [E04] [E11] [S03] [S04]

**Sufficient aggregate admission rule.** For each phase `p`, establish a bound `F_p` for other retained owners, a live/enforced allowance `C_p` for the active child, and an independently justified maximum `S_p` for scratch/old-new overlap not charged by that child. Require

\[
F_p+C_p+S_p\le B.
\]

Every growth operation must be covered by the child's pre-execution cap or the phase reservation, including operations between public checkpoints. Ownership transitions require their own overlap bound. If these premises hold for all phases, then `M(t)≤B` throughout the invocation. This is elementary accounting, not a new solver theorem.

A valid conservative bound can be larger than any simultaneously observed live total. Report it as an **accounting upper bound**, not as a measured peak. The sum of unrelated component maxima is also not an observed simultaneous peak, although it can contribute to a conservative upper bound if the component domains are disjoint and exhaustive.

**Counterexample to sampled peak claims.** At a yield, a vector owns `m` bytes. Growing it temporarily retains its old `m` bytes while allocating `2m` new bytes, then releases the old block before the next yield. Two checkpoint samples can observe `m` and `2m`, missing the `3m` overlap. Sampling more often at entry boundaries cannot prove the overlap never occurs. The same issue applies to parsed graph plus source JSON, moved/copied result storage, serializers and exception cleanup.

### 6.5 Borrowed census and safe release proof

Let `R` be the complete evaluator result and `C` its census subobject. The validator stores a reference to `C`, not an owning copy. Safety requires the owning `R` object, the referenced subobject and its transitive storage to remain valid for the full validator lifetime; other problem/control/price references have the same requirement.

The current sufficient discipline is simple: retain the checker and its completed output; construct the validator; advance it; destroy the validator and its suspended/native children; only then move or destroy the checker result and surrounding dependencies. This matches the inspected consumer order. [S01] [S06] [S07]

An alternative stable-result owner can be safe, but only if installed before borrowing begins, with no later moves or vector erasures that invalidate the reference. Move the **complete** result—not a hand-selected census projection—and preserve full graph/provenance/economic identity for later publication. Charge the transition's peak overlap. Transferring ownership does not erase already executed checker work. The C++ Core Guidelines' RAII and non-owning-reference guidance supports this engineering discipline; it does not establish this repository's memory bound or certify its native policies. [X01]
