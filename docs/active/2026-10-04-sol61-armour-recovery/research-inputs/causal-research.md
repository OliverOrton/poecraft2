## 7. Mathematical argument and its native obligations

### 7.1 Problem identity and policy state

Let the physical problem identity be

\[
\mathcal I=(x_0,G,\mathcal D,\mathcal A,\mathcal P,\mathcal O,p),
\]

where \(x_0\) is the exact incoming native root; \(G\) is the original rarity/tier/clean terminal predicate; \(\mathcal D\) is frozen native data and law; \(\mathcal A\) is the caller action scope; \(\mathcal P\) is the admitted programme grammar with mandatory commitments; \(\mathcal O\) is permitted observation timing; and \(p\) is the price map. The resource/profile/numerical identity \(\mathcal B\) additionally describes how a bounded search or checker is run. Changing \(\mathcal B\) changes the delivery experiment even when it leaves the physical control problem unchanged.

A finite controller must be evaluated on a state space containing the exact relevant native item **and** controller node/phase, plus active checkpoint or observation memory. Two occurrences with the same displayed goal mask are not necessarily interchangeable. They may have different inferior-tier blockers, crafted identities, dominance, affix capacity, legal next actions or mandatory programme commitments. Common finite-domain reasoning below can use disjoint controller-tagged states; it does not require an approximate native state merge. [D04], [D01]

### 7.2 Fixed-policy value and executable upper

**Conditional proposition, already owned by CLM-0002.** Fix a finite, complete, legal controller \(\pi\). Let \(Q_\pi\) be its substochastic transition matrix among nonterminal reachable states and \(c_\pi\) its finite expected immediate cost vector. Assume the controller reaches the original goal almost surely from every entry for which authority is requested. Then the corresponding reachable nonterminal chain is transient,

\[
Q_\pi^n\to0,\qquad Z_\pi=(I-Q_\pi)^{-1}=\sum_{n\ge0}Q_\pi^n,
\]

and

\[
J_\pi=Z_\pi c_\pi=c_\pi+Q_\pi J_\pi.
\]

The series sums expected cost at each successive decision. Nonnegative costs permit termwise expectation; finite transience and finite rewards give a finite value. If the controller is allowed under \(\mathcal I\), it is one feasible policy, so

\[
V^*_{\mathcal I}(x_0)\le J_\pi(x_0).
\]

This is an entry-scoped feasible-policy argument, not an optimality proof. A finite numerical solution alone does not establish its premises: a zero-cost non-goal self-loop satisfies \(J=J\) for every finite scalar and never succeeds. [D04], [T01]

**Native proof gap for the private 210k graph.** Its root evaluator reports success 1 and a complete diagnostic cost, but the native programme-admission contract has not been discharged for all positive reached entries. Evaluating the emitted physical graph and proving that each dependency-only programme occurrence is authorized by the original native solver scope are distinct obligations. The unvalidated entries are not harmless merely because the root evaluator has finished. Conversely, this does not demand properness at every unreachable physical item in the game; it demands the contract's actual positive reached entries. [S15], [D01]

### 7.3 Complete paid programme composition

Let a native word or local controller \(w\), entered at a legitimate decision state \(x\), run until its allowed exit boundary. Suppose it has almost-sure finite exit, finite expected paid reward \(b_w(x)\), and complete exit kernel \(K_w(x,y)\). Mandatory acquisition/setup/cleanup/recovery and observation timing are included inside these objects.

If every positive exit has a compatible route in a fixed proper continuation controller \(\pi\), taking \(w\) once and then following \(\pi\) has value

\[
Q_\pi(x,w)=b_w(x)+\sum_y K_w(x,y)J_\pi(y).
\]

This follows by conditioning on the complete first exit. Internal cost and the exit may be correlated; the expectation remains valid because the continuation value is conditioned on the actual exit state. The formula grants no right to decide in the middle of a mandatory option. It also does not prove the recurrent controller obtained by selecting \(w\) on every revisit is proper. [D04], [T02]

For the partial-held witness, the complete Ember/Exalt native word supplies a local law. To conclude a cheaper original-root policy, all 33 exits must be connected to legal, priced, compatible continuations and the resulting complete controller must pass native checking. An exit absent from the historical reachable domain is an **unknown continuation**, not the historical root cost, a saved lower, or zero.

Two locally terminating options \(x\to y\) and \(y\to x\) can compose into a controller which never reaches the goal. Therefore local word termination is necessary evidence but not sufficient global properness. This is the existing CLM-0004 composition obligation; its broad native correspondence remains open. [D04], [D05]

### 7.4 Occupancy and resource attribution

For root selector \(e_0\), expected nonterminal visits satisfy

\[
d_\pi^\top=e_0^\top Z_\pi,
\qquad J_\pi(x_0)=d_\pi^\top c_\pi.
\]

Let \(r_k(x)\) be expected consumption of resource \(k\) per represented decision. Its root expected consumption is \(d_\pi^\top r_k\), and the price-weighted sum must reconcile with monetary value:

\[
J_\pi(x_0)=\sum_k p_k\,d_\pi^\top r_k.
\]

An occupancy can exceed one because the controller retries. Thus expected visits are not probabilities of ever using an action. A stage's root-weighted expenditure is not the conditional expected cost of starting at that stage. Nor can overlapping continuation values be added as if they were independent stage expenditures. [D04]

The recorded action decomposition in section 6 is useful for choosing investigations and detecting accounting inconsistencies. It is not a counterfactual calculation. The tiny positive ordering mass in the partial-held fixture proves reachability; it is neither total class reachability nor the first-disagreement probability needed to attribute a root gap. Multiplying it by 85,970 or by the Chaos-spend difference would have no established causal meaning.

### 7.5 Policy-change identity and a sufficient improvement condition

Assume two complete proper controllers \(\pi\) and \(\mu\) are represented on a common legitimately aligned finite domain. Define the advantage of \(\mu\)'s decision against \(\pi\)'s continuation values by

\[
A_\mu^\pi=c_\mu+Q_\mu J_\pi-J_\pi.
\]

Subtracting their fixed-policy equations gives

\[
(I-Q_\mu)(J_\mu-J_\pi)=A_\mu^\pi,
\]

so

\[
J_\mu-J_\pi=(I-Q_\mu)^{-1}A_\mu^\pi.
\]

The transient inverse is nonnegative. Therefore nonpositive advantage on the relevant domain is sufficient for non-increase; a strictly negative component reached with positive expected visits under \(\mu\) gives strict root improvement. The condition is sufficient, not necessary. A fully evaluated candidate may improve despite some positive local advantages. [D04]

Two cautions are decisive here. First, the root change is weighted by the **new** controller's occupancy, not the old graph's action counts. Second, properness is an explicit premise. Against a cost-10 finishing action, a zero-cost self-loop can have zero one-step advantage while eliminating termination. A ranking calculation is not permission to publish that loop.

Native application requires compatible state/controller memory, complete native coefficients, correctly timed choices, all paid resources and an independently evaluated actual emitted graph. None of those bridges is supplied by the arithmetic identity alone.

### 7.6 First-disagreement attribution: the smallest meaningful economic witness

A more direct causal decomposition is possible when two complete controllers share a truly aligned prefix from the original root. Stop that common process at the goal or its first legitimate decision disagreement. Let \(\nu(d)\) be the subprobability of first reaching disagreement boundary \(d\). Let \(V_A(d)\) and \(V_B(d)\) be the **full** remaining values, including subsequent returns, for the two controllers.

Conditioning on that first exit cancels the common prefix reward:

\[
J_A(x_0)-J_B(x_0)
 =\sum_d\nu(d)\,[V_A(d)-V_B(d)].
\]

For a finite cyclic common prefix, its stopped substochastic matrix and exit equations define \(\nu\); cycles do not authorize replacing first-entry mass by ordinary occupancy. Paths reaching the goal before disagreement contribute zero. The argument requires common law, root, observations and legitimate decision boundaries, not just the same item picture. [D04]

This is the right target for a tightly scoped **economic** witness. The partial-held fixture currently proves one positive reachable disagreement, but not the complete disagreement distribution or both complete tails. If some tails remain unbound, retain their explicit unknown contribution. Do not normalize the known subset to mass one.

### 7.7 Complementary decisions: why one restored opcode can fail economically

A synthetic deterministic example illustrates the limitation of testing only isolated edits. The incumbent chooses \(r\to g\) for cost 10 and, at an otherwise unvisited state \(t\), chooses \(t\to g\) for cost 100. Available alternatives are \(r\to t\) for cost 1 and a different \(t\to g\) action for cost 1.

| Controller change | Root value |
|---|---:|
| None | 10 |
| Bridge only | 101 |
| Tail only | 10 |
| Bridge and tail | 2 |

All four controllers are proper. A proposal filter demanding strict immediate root improvement from each isolated edit can reject both ingredients even though the combined controller is cheap. This is not a failure of full-domain policy iteration; it is a limitation of that bounded construction/filtering procedure. [D04]

For Conquest, it justifies allowing **one finite, evidence-selected closure of dependent decisions**, not an open-ended search for arbitrary new grammar families. It does not prove that the historical partial-held word needs any particular blocked or plain-fill tail. The latter is a native hypothesis and must be checked.

### 7.8 Bounds, ordering, scope extension and exact closure

A checked fixed policy gives an upper. A whole-MDP lower requires a valid lower argument over **all** allowed alternatives. A restricted search's optimum, an old root scalar, a local continuation estimate or a fixed-policy lower endpoint is not automatically such a lower. A heuristic may prioritize work without gaining exclusion authority. [D02], [D06]

If a programme scope is widened, an old policy that remains legal can remain an upper. An old lower need not remain valid: the new scope may contain a cheaper policy. This explains why simply restoring positive ordinary-clean proof authority to the current product request is not a safe way to recover old scheduling. The constructor's neutral profile is a correctness boundary, not merely an inconvenient display choice. [S12], [D01]

The exact-closure conclusion requires compatible valid endpoints and full alternative coverage:

\[
L(x_0)\le V^*(x_0)\le J_\pi(x_0)\le U(x_0).
\]

Equality of mathematically guaranteed endpoints fixes the optimum. The repository's finite numerical reconciliation contract must still be stated precisely; a displayed decimal is not silently promoted to an outward-rounded symbolic upper. The 101k and historical 85,970 values are feasible-policy/evaluation evidence, not global optimality proofs. [D02]

### 7.9 Numerical falsification and positive-mass obligations

For a proper fixed policy and approximate vector \(v\),

\[
J-v=(I-Q)^{-1}(c+Qv-v).
\]

A small residual can be amplified by a large expected retry count. In the one-state example with success probability \(10^{-6}\), a residual of \(10^{-6}\) can correspond to a value error of 1. Therefore a tiny residual is not evidence that another candidate cannot improve, and it does not supply missing programme admission. [D02]

The witness's mass `1.0000000000000002` passes its named `1e-12` mass tolerance. It is not a reason to change the native law or introduce normalization of an incomplete row. A positive entry, even one reached through a very small probability, remains an obligation. In the private real-root check, 1,717 accepted entries out of 12,658 cannot be extrapolated into a checked upper.

### 7.10 Resource accounting: a separate engineering argument

Let simultaneous live storage be partitioned into disjoint owners \(M_i(t)\), with explicitly reserved growth/scratch \(R(t)\). The relevant aggregate boundary is based on

\[
\sup_t\left[\sum_i M_i(t)+R(t)\right],
\]

within the implementation's declared accounting model. A census already included in a live checker is not an extra independent allocation. A post-release sample cannot establish the peak while old and new storage overlap. A faster observation routine does not prove complete transient accounting. [D07]

The recorded checker live estimate of 87,596,436 bytes includes a 54,621,231-byte result/census subset. Their difference, 32,975,205 bytes, is at most a preliminary removable-storage estimate before ownership transfer and overlap, not a measured saving or a proof that all remaining entries fit. The 141.157-second to 0.787-second observer result establishes the cost of that observation change on that graph. It does not establish a new policy, product speedup, or cause of an earlier cap-free product result. [S15], [S22]

Committed native work is cumulative across suspension, refusal, rollback and destruction. Cached kernel reuse may legitimately execute zero new work; an uncached query membership is not proof of an uncached kernel. These distinctions explain some invalid private fixture premises without excusing actual unowned work in the separate Finder witnesses. [S11], [D07]
