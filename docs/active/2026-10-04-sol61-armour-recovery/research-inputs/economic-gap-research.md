## 5. Mathematical argument and explicit proof boundaries

### 5.1 Fixed-policy value and occupancy

Let an execution state include the exact native item and every future-observable control component: programme counter, implicit tiers, checkpoint and observed-choice memory where applicable. Let `G` be the true requested clean goal. For a fixed finite controller π, restrict to its root-reachable nonterminal states. Let `Qπ` be its complete transition matrix on those states, `rπ` its expected immediate paid cost, and `α` the original-root distribution.

Assume complete native rows, allowed information timing, all mandatory prices/resources, almost-sure absorption in `G`, finite expected cost, and a compatible numerical interpretation. For finite transient `Qπ`,

\[
J_\pi=r_\pi+Q_\pi J_\pi,
\quad J_\pi=(I-Q_\pi)^{-1}r_\pi,
\quad d_\pi^T=\alpha^T(I-Q_\pi)^{-1}.
\]

The inverse is the nonnegative Neumann series. Therefore total root cost is `dπᵀrπ`. Resource quantity `k` is `dπᵀgπ,k`, where `g` counts **immediate** native resource use. It must not be a node's complete cost-to-go; summing occupancy times cost-to-go repeatedly counts later work. These are the established fixed-policy/flow arguments, not a novelty claim. [D01] (sections `fixed-policy`, `policy-difference`) [D02] (sections `flow-accounting`)

If the graph is a fully admitted executable member of the original policy class, its cost supplies an entry-scoped feasible upper on that class's optimum. A numerical root result for a generated programme graph does not itself establish all native entry admissions. Conversely, an authored historical graph may be evaluable while lacking the provenance needed for a current solver incumbent. The private 210k number and historical 85.97k reference occupy those different boundaries.

A zero-cost non-goal self-loop shows why unrestricted raw accumulated cost and proper-policy cost cannot be silently identified. Root-unreachable bad components also show why one root certificate is not a universal arbitrary-entry certificate. General SSP theory makes the proper-policy distinction; it does not verify poecraft2's native correspondence. [D04] (sections CLM-0001, CLM-0002) [T01]

### 5.2 Stage accounting and complete stopped summaries

Choose a legal boundary set `B`, retaining exact physical and control identity. Let `R` be a transient interior of a **fixed selected** stage/excursion, stopped at the first boundary hit or true goal. Then

\[
F=(I-Q_{RR})^{-1},\qquad b=Fr_R,\qquad H=FQ_{RB},\qquad J_R=b+HJ_B.
\]

This follows by solving `J_R=r_R+Q_RR J_R+Q_RB J_B`. The stopped cost `b` includes every internal retry and setup that actually repeats. `H` retains all positive exit mass and its exact entry identity. Terminal reward/categories must also be retained; a diagnostic STOP or off-policy failure is not the true goal. [D01] (sections `programs`, `return-bridges`, `boundary-response`)

The table in §3 uses `d_Rᵀr_R`, a **root occupancy attribution**, not `b`. A stage can be entered more than once and from several contexts. To compute a replacement's value, bind its complete `H` to actual continuation values or solve the full composed controller. Two locally terminating options, one taking `s→t` and the other `t→s`, form an improper global composition. Local finite exits alone cannot supply a full-root upper. [D04] (sections CLM-0004)

An action table, a stage table, and a “number of goals at execution” table are different partitions or cross-classifications of the same rewards. Add only disjoint cells of one partition. To combine axes, first form disjoint intersections `(stage, action, exact observation class)` and sum each cell once. Root visit counts, conditional entry probabilities and repeated execution counts are not interchangeable.

### 5.3 Renewal with paid recovery and actual return states

For identical independent trials that really return to the same priced state, a trial of expected cost `c`, success probability `p>0`, and failure recovery cost `d` obeys

\[
V=c+(1-p)(d+V)
=\frac{c+(1-p)d}{p}.
\]

A synthetic example with `c=1`, `p=1/128`, `d=2` gives 382, not the free-recovery answer 128. The example is not a native forecast. If a complete cycle contains acquisition cost `a` and tail cost `b`, both stopping at the true goal or the **same** retry entry, then `V=(a+b)/p`. Do not add the tail's full recurring value again after already including it in `b`. [D01] (sections `retry`)

For several retry states, write instead

\[
v_i=c_i+\sum_j T_{ij}v_j,\qquad v=(I-T)^{-1}c,
\]

where each `c_i` is the cost until the next declared boundary and `T` includes all returns, not just failures carrying a familiar goal mask. In this graph, paid Chaos re-acquisition preserves actual implicit tiers. A failure in frame `(1,2)` does not return for free to the original `(0,0)` root. One scalar “success per attempt” is unjustified unless a true renewal equivalence has been shown. Exact native distinctions may share computation only under the existing equality contract, not approximate merging.

### 5.4 Acquisition acceptance must include continuation economics

A useful restricted formula makes the tradeoff explicit. Suppose each acquisition attempt from a genuine renewal state costs `c`; it immediately reaches the true goal with probability `p0` or produces observable carriers `i` with probabilities `w_i`. Rejecting a carrier costs `r` and returns to that same renewal state. For accepted carrier `i`, a selected complete excursion costs `a_i`, reaches goal with probability `p_i`, and on failure requires recovery `r` before returning to the same renewal state. Let `S` be the accepted carrier set. Then

\[
V(S)=c+\sum_{i\notin S}w_i(r+V)
 +\sum_{i\in S}w_i\{a_i+(1-p_i)(r+V)\},
\]

hence

\[
V(S)=
\frac{c+\sum_{i\notin S}w_i r+
\sum_{i\in S}w_i[a_i+(1-p_i)r]}
{p_0+\sum_{i\in S}w_i p_i}.
\]

Here `p0+Σwi=1`, and the denominator must be positive. Carrier-dependent recovery replaces `r` with its actual expected cost/kernel. Different tier/rarity/blocker returns require the matrix equation, not this scalar shortcut. This derivation is a conditional model, **not a fitted estimate of the Conquest values**.

The relevant comparison at an observed carrier is the complete accepted excursion versus paid rejection and continuation. It is not merely “probability of one useful mod divided by Chaos price.” A synthetic acquisition costing 1 with a mandatory finishing cost 100 is worse than acquisition costing 10 with finishing cost 20, despite being ten times cheaper to obtain. Private Chaos spend of 44.66 is an observed reward total, not an independently optimized acquisition objective.

### 5.5 Observed branching and policy improvement

When `X` has already been observed and the native rules allow a decision, the optimal local choice among complete alternatives is

\[
\mathbb E[\min_{a\in A(X)}q(X,a)],
\]

not `min_a E[q(X,a)]`. For equiprobable observations with action costs `(0,10)` and `(10,0)`, those expressions are 0 and 5. A controller may inspect the actual result after a legitimate programme boundary. It may not choose after seeing an outcome that the native commitment hides, or switch within a mandatory word solely to improve a calculation. Evaluation follows the graph's fixed branching rule; it must not opportunistically replace it by newer solver decisions. [D01] (sections `choices`, `programs`) [D04] (sections CLM-0003)

On a common complete finite domain where both controllers are proper and `Jπ` is genuinely defined at all relevant successors, define

\[
A_\pi^\mu=r_\mu+Q_\mu J_\pi-J_\pi.
\]

Subtract the Bellman equations to obtain

\[
J_\mu-J_\pi=(I-Q_\mu)^{-1}A_\pi^\mu,
\quad J_\mu(\alpha)-J_\pi(\alpha)=d_\mu^T A_\pi^\mu.
\]

The **new controller's occupancy** appears. Old action spend alone is not the gain from replacing that action. Componentwise `A≤0` is a sufficient improvement argument under the full premises; it is not necessary for a root improvement, and unknown exit continuations make `A` unknown rather than expensive. A one-time deviation also differs from a permanent recurring replacement unless the extra control memory is represented. [D01] (sections `policy-difference`, `improvement`)

An exact synthetic illustration: let the old controller have `Jπ=(12,10)`, the new one `Jμ=(6,5)`, advantage `(-1,-5)`, old root occupancy `(2,1)`, and new occupancy `(1,1)`. For example, old `s` costs 1 and moves half to `s`, half to `t`, with `t` finishing at cost 10; new `s` costs 1 then moves to `t`, whose finish costs 5. The true difference is −6. Multiplying by old occupancy incorrectly predicts −7. These are synthetic coefficients, not native data.

### 5.6 Conditional proposition: deleting an annul that a reroll completely erases

**Proposition.** At a specified legitimate entry `x`, let `A` be a paid Annul word and `C` the following paid reroll word. Suppose:

1. Both old `A;C` and proposed `C` are fully legal, with correct setup and resource costs.
2. Every positive exit of `A` follows `C`; there is no intervening success or different observed choice.
3. As kernels on the **full post-word item/control state**,
   `K_C(x,·)=Σ_y K_A(x,y) K_C(y,·)`.
4. The same complete proper continuation follows both words, and the reroll/setup cost law agrees.

Then deleting `A` preserves the embedded post-word transition law and removes exactly the expected cost of `A` per visit to that entry. A recurring replacement preserves the embedded chain and therefore its properness; total cost reduction is the resulting expected number of such macro visits times the removed cost (summed over compatible entries).

**Proof.** Condition on the Annul exit. Premise 3 gives the same next-boundary distribution for both words; premise 4 gives identical expected continuation value. Subtract the immediate cost terms. Iterating this equality at every visit leaves the embedded chain unchanged, so its occupation measure at those boundaries is unchanged as well. This is a special kernel-equality argument; it is not the generally invalid subtraction of old marginal action spend.

**Candidate native application.** In the ready `(1,2)` final frame, consider three T1 prefixes and three ordinary suffixes, none satisfying either requested suffix goal, with no target fractures/crafts or extra persistent state affecting the law. `c81→c94` annuls to two unwanted suffixes, after which `c96/c95` choose `c88` Chaos. The documented dominant reroll clears those remaining suffixes and draws from the preserved three-prefix state. This strongly motivates equality, but the exact native comparison has **not** been run here. At an initial frame, both alternatives must still pay the identical Ichor setup. [E04] [D08]

**Falsification conditions.** A requested suffix can be lost or a goal reached after Annul; a fracture can survive reroll; a target-side persistent effect can change pool weights; an intervening observation may select a different continuation; or setup may differ. Any of these can invalidate a premise. The proposed guard must refuse such states unless separately proved. The aggregate `c94` spend includes more than the no-goal subset, so its entire 15,695.54 is **not** the proposed saving.

### 5.7 Why a one-word historical graft can fail even with perfect native support

On the P0 partial carrier, Ember followed by prefix Eldritch Exalt preserves the lower-tier suppression suffix. Every exit still lacks T1 suppression, so returning immediately to the released router sends every exit to Chaos. The historical word is useful only with a compatible different continuation, not automatically as a one-step insertion. This routing statement follows from the witnessed word preservation and released suffix tests. [S08] [E17]

A stronger **conditional** calculation is available. Assume native coupling verifies that the subsequent ordinary Chaos wipes the same ordinary explicit state and gives identical explicit-result laws in tier frames `(0,0)` and `(1,0)`, and that the only downstream difference is advancing the released controller's eventual one-time Ember setup. If that later Ember would occur with probability `q≤1`, the inserted word's incremental cost is

\[
(0.1563+3.59)-0.1563q\ \ge\ 3.59.
\]

Thus the naive one-word graft is non-improving under those premises. This is an analytical falsification attempt, not a newly measured native Q value. Native pool/control equality remains its proof gap. It does not refute the joint partial-held-plus-tail proposal: that joint controller deliberately changes the continuation. Do not use a failed isolated graft to prune a complementary combined proposal from the original action space.

### 5.8 Numerical acceptance, bounds and exact closure

For approximate fixed-policy values `v`, residual `e=r+Qv−v` obeys

\[
J-v=(I-Q)^{-1}e.
\]

Small local residuals can be amplified by long recurrence. The original epsilon and reconciliation tolerances must remain unchanged; calling evaluation “exact” does not make the printed decimal a rigorously outward-rounded rational endpoint. A small fixed-policy lower endpoint is also not a global MDP lower. [D02] (sections `layers`, `residual`, `exactness`)

A real economic pass requires a fully accepted new controller under matching identity, with its complete price and entry contract, cheaper than the qualified control beyond the existing numerical comparison rules. Global closure additionally requires a valid original-scope lower and all allowed alternatives accounted for. No stage score, renewal heuristic, historical policy price, or resource refusal supplies that lower. The released run's lower is zero; this packet changes neither it nor exactness authority.
