# Generic candidate construction: a bounded composition audit

**Repository:** OliverOrton/poecraft2  
**Programme:** Question 2, “Improve generic candidate construction”  
**Research basis:** remote source and published receipts inspected on October 4, 2026  
**Authority:** research and proposed implementation only. No execution is authorized by this packet.

## 1. Executive verdict

**The complete-held-side grammar is a demonstrated local construction restriction. It is not a demonstrated complete explanation of the released Conquest cost gap, nor is it evidence that Current lost its Eldritch Exalt primitive.** Native support and compilation admit more than the bounded producer currently constructs. The strongest witness is a positively reachable four-affix carrier on which historical routing uses a paid Ember → Eldritch Exalt word while the saved released controller uses Chaos. The private producer constructs that word, but its resulting whole controller is substantially more expensive and still lacks complete positive-entry admission. [S04, S06, S11, S12]

The important distinction is therefore not simply **narrow grammar versus broad grammar**. It is:

> Which complete, legal, paid, proper continuations does the builder construct; which does the real consumer attempt; and which survive checking and improve the original-root incumbent within the unchanged resource envelope?

The private fixed-stage design demonstrates why these questions differ. Retaining a singleton suffix almost whenever it appears makes initial acquisition cheap, but commits the controller to expensive subsequent side rerolls. Its three-prefix-held finishing stage is also expensive. Adding generic final Exalt fill greatly improves that isolated candidate, without making it competitive with the released winner. The next construction change should introduce **bounded alternatives to those commitments**, not indiscriminately add more stages. [S03, S04]

### Recommended implementation cut

Use the existing native word, finite-control, compiler, evaluator, entry-validator and incumbent owners to implement a **bounded guarded-deviation portfolio**:

1. A partial-held growth alternative that permits an admitted paid Exalt word without committing every subsequent singleton-anchor state to repeated Eldritch Chaos.
2. A finishing alternative that compares complete plain-fill and native temporary-blocker-fill controllers, with every miss, cleanup and recovery retained.
3. Their joint composition, because individually unprofitable or root-unreachable edits can be complementary.

This is a small search over complete controllers, not a new MDP representation, recipe registry, supervisor, learned pruning system, or global policy-iteration engine. Candidate ranking may be heuristic; acceptance remains unchanged. A prototype should offer **at most six newly constructed controller candidates, and at most two new complete admission attempts per timed case**, under the existing aggregate limits. The precise portfolio and scheduling rules are in Sections 6–7. Those proposed limits are not restored authorization to run anything.

**Expected benefit is a hypothesis, not a promised result.** The economic gate is a cheaper fully checked policy for the unchanged original Conquest request, plus demonstrated improvement on at least two of the four retained additional armour cases. Bow4, Bow5, Ring and Amulet remain controls. The historical 85,970.67347138176 reference is a recovery target, not an admitted product upper supplied by this research. Failure to meet the economic gate must not be relabelled as recovery because a local word becomes constructible or a checker becomes faster.

### 4.1 Existing stage and word machinery

The main owner is `engine/src/solver_selective_completion.cpp`, with contracts in its header and `solver_compile_contracts.hpp`. It already separates candidate construction from independent acceptance. It proposes native held contexts for admission rather than pretending those proposal items were reached from the original root. The mapping for an additional blocker variant exists, but the normal count does not enumerate that diagnostic fourth proposal. The complete-held gate has two layers. `begin()` derives the held mask from all requested goals on the selected side, and growth requires a two-held/three-target all-goal shape. `build()` sends failure of any held-slot test to acquisition before its growth branch. A clean held side with excess occupancy also routes to acquisition. A legal partial-held native word cannot pass this particular producer gate simply because the underlying mechanic exists. [S11]

The native intent helper supports partial-held Eldritch Exalt subject to native applicability, preserved goals and rollability. ### 4.2 Private composition improves coverage but commits too early

The private `PartialHeldRecoveryProducer` constructs four sequential stage templates: singleton-held growth, complete-two-side growth, and complete-three-side finishing at two source tier frames. It keeps at most one active admission cursor. It derives two singleton anchors from the two-goal side, not from a base name, modifier key, historical graph or fixture ID. Its scope is deliberately narrow: empty ordinary live Rare roots, native side capacity three, five distinct natural family goals in a pure 3+2 shape, clean target, representable tiers and the required count-law support. [S14]

After mandatory paid Chaos acquisition, central dispatch selects a complete-side stage, a singleton stage, or reacquisition. This provides the proven partial word, but also commits many cheap-to-acquire singleton carriers to repeated expensive side work. The measured cost is evidence against that particular persistent commitment, not against keeping incomplete sides in general. [S03, S14]

The tier handling is bounded, not a theorem that switching sides is harmless. Setup words determine ready tiers. The composer retains original, growth-ready, and corresponding finishing frames, with refusal ports for unsupported frames. Chaos preserves tier identity: a paid whole-item reforge does not return to the original zero-tier root. Any proposed additional switch must either stay within proved complete frame closure or explicitly construct and pay for the new continuation. [S14]

## 5. Mathematical argument and its exact limits

This section applies the repository's existing policy/programme framework. It is not a claim of a new theorem. Definitions and algebra below are explanatory derivations; empirical premises are separately cited.

### 5.1 Problem and policy identity

Write the immutable problem identity as

\[
\theta=(x_0,G,\mathcal A,\mathcal D,\mathcal P,\mathcal L,\mathcal N),
\]

where `x0` is the full original native item, `G` is the exact native clean/coverage goal predicate, `A` is the requested/admitted action and programme scope, `D` is frozen native data, `P` is the frozen price/resource map, `L` is the law/version and `N` is the numerical acceptance contract. Runtime limits and scheduling are additionally part of the experiment identity. A family-goal mask, current affix count, “same base” label or scalar root cost is not a replacement for this identity.

A controller state is `(x,m)`: the exact action-relevant physical item plus finite control memory, including phase, selected continuation and any attempt counter. Required distinctions include goal tiers, natural/crafted/fractured identity, capacity, full modifier exclusions, influence/metamod/eldritch state and every other field observed by admitted actions. A new internal phase is not a free physical reset. A shared code template is not permission to merge probability mass.

Let `S` be the finite reachable nonterminal controller-state set of a fully constructed policy. Let `Q` be its substochastic transition matrix on `S`, with goal exits removed from `Q` but not from the underlying probability law. Let `c(s)` be expected immediate paid cost, and `b_r(s)` the expected debit of resource `r`. Every positive native outcome must appear, including goal loss, non-goal failure, self-loops and recovery.

If the nonterminal chain is transient and the rewards are finite,

\[
J=(I-Q)^{-1}c,\qquad
B_r=(I-Q)^{-1}b_r,\qquad
d^{\mathsf T}=e_{s_0}^{\mathsf T}(I-Q)^{-1}.
\]

Thus `J(s0)=Σ_s d(s)c(s)`. Root-weighted stage spend is a sum of such occupancy-weighted rewards. It is not the cost conditional on entering that stage, the probability of ever using it, or the saving obtained by deleting it. This is precisely the distinction needed to interpret Section 3. [S17, S18]

A legal, proper complete policy supplies a feasible upper for its identical problem: `V*(s0) ≤ J(s0)`. This mathematical statement does not elevate an incompletely admitted numerical estimate into the product's checked-upper class. Nor does it establish a global lower bound, unrestricted optimality or an exact-solver closure claim.

### 5.2 Composition of complete native words

A native word `w` starts at an admitted entry `s`, executes to a stopping time `τ`, and emits an observable exit `z`. Define

\[
C_w(s)=\mathbb E_s\!\left[\sum_{t<\tau}c_t\right],\quad
R_{w,r}(s)=\mathbb E_s\!\left[\sum_{t<\tau}b_{t,r}\right],\quad
K_w(s,z)=\Pr_s(S_\tau=z).
\]

The entry contract must specify native legality at every intermediate step, information available to each decision, complete positive exits, and all mandatory setup/cleanup/recovery. It must also establish the stopping and expected-reward conditions needed for the word model. A bench → Exalt → remove-craft attempt includes the removal even when the Exalt hits the requested goal, unless the native programme explicitly specifies a different complete legal branch. A “hit” cannot bypass a mandatory paid operation.

For a second word chosen after the first observable exit, with compatible entry domain, composition is

\[
C_{12}(s)=C_1(s)+\sum_zK_1(s,z)C_{2,z}(z),
\]

\[
R_{12,r}(s)=R_{1,r}(s)+\sum_zK_1(s,z)R_{2,z,r}(z),
\]

\[
K_{12}(s,y)=\sum_zK_1(s,z)K_{2,z}(z,y).
\]

These follow by conditioning on the first stopping state and the tower property. Every positive first exit participates. An unsupported exit cannot be discarded and the remaining probabilities cannot be renormalized. If a continuation is selected before an outcome is observed, the selector must remain fixed through that outcome: `min E` and `E min` are different decision models. [S17]

For a checked continuation value `H` on all positive exits,

\[
Q_w^H(s)=C_w(s)+\sum_zK_w(s,z)H(z)
\]

is a valid one-word-plus-tail value under the stated assumptions. A scalar original-root cost, an MDP lower bound, an ordering score or a value at the wrong tier/control port is not `H(z)`. Where the compatible tail domain is incomplete, this comparison is unavailable. The initial implementation need not add a new tail-value service: evaluating whole candidate controllers avoids inventing these values.

**Proof gap that remains empirical:** Native correctness of a new emitted word and its complete entry domain is not proved by the equations. It must come from the existing native enumerator, compiler and positive-entry validation on the unchanged problem.

### 5.3 Local word termination is not global policy properness

Two words can each terminate in one step and still alternate forever: word A moves to B's entry, and word B moves back to A's entry, with no goal exit. Making either word cheap does not solve this. The complete composed controller must have no reachable non-goal recurrent class.

For a finite fully known positive-support controller, almost-sure goal absorption is equivalent to every reachable bottom strongly connected component being a true goal component. This is a qualitative graph property. A tiny positive escape or failure edge cannot be dropped because it lies below a convenient numerical threshold. With finite rewards, finite transience also gives finite expected accumulated cost. In the general SSP setting, improper policies and infinite-cost issues require care; Bertsekas's proper-policy treatment supports this distinction, not any PoE-specific claim. [S17, S25]

There is one useful sufficient construction pattern. Suppose at most `k` intervention words execute, all have finite expected duration/reward, and every possible subsequent state belongs to the properly checked fallback domain. After the budget expires, execution permanently follows that fallback. Then the intervention prefix has finite expectation, followed by a proper finite-value tail, so the whole policy is proper. The compatible-domain premise is indispensable.

That argument **does not apply** merely because one Exalt is allowed per acquisition episode: if paid reacquisition resets the intervention budget, there can be infinitely many episodes. The proposed repeatable controller therefore still requires full original-root and entry checking. Finite control size is not itself a proof of termination.

### 5.4 Full groups, partial sides and the two blocker tests

Let `H` be the retained side and `T` the target side. Let `M_T(s)` be the unsatisfied target goals at state `s`. Let `E_g(s)` be the native positive-weight members that satisfy goal `g` after all relevant eligibility, tier, group, capacity and scope conditions.

The missing-goal-rollability predicate has the form

\[
\operatorname{Rollable}(s,T)
\iff \exists g\in M_T(s):E_g(s)\ne\varnothing.
\]

This is a progress predicate, not a general legality predicate. The real negative fixture demonstrated an admitted Eldritch Exalt with an empty eligible result becoming a paid self-loop. Calling that action illegal would misstate the native law. Calling it a useful growth step would also be wrong. [S04, S07]

Now clear the target side in a **diagnostic eligibility view**, retaining the surviving side's exact groups and other relevant fields. A necessary retained-side compatibility test is

\[
\operatorname{Compatible}(s,H,T)
\implies
\forall g\in G_T:\ E_g(\operatorname{clear}_T(s))\ne\varnothing.
\]

The diagnostic clear operation is not an executable free action. It asks whether a paid target reroll could in principle unblock goals without modifying the retained side. It must include target goals that are already satisfied: a reroll may destroy them, after which they must be reacquired.

Three counterexamples delimit the guards:

**Target-side blocker.** A lower-tier member occupies the desired higher-tier member's exclusion family on the target side. Exalt cannot replace it; a legal target-side reroll or annul may remove it. That repair is paid and may lose useful target goals. Exalt looping without the guard can leave a closed or very expensive class.

**Cross-side retained blocker.** A retained modifier has a secondary native group in common with every eligible member of a missing target goal, even though its primary group differs. Clearing/rerolling the target side leaves the obstruction. The retained-blocker fixture exposed this: target-only guarding still left about 0.840018823 unresolved probability. The correction used permitted, paid whole-item Chaos recovery, not a free target reset. [S04, S07]

**Joint-feasibility counterexample, synthetic.** Target goal A has one candidate and target goal B has another. Each is individually compatible with the retained side, but the two candidates share an exclusion group and cannot coexist. The individual compatibility test passes while the conjunction of goals is impossible in that class. Therefore the guard is a useful necessary filter, not a replacement for exact goal matching, native law closure or controller validation.

### 5.5 “Disposable junk” must have an executable disposal policy

A non-goal modifier is disposable only relative to a complete admitted continuation. Its current lack of goal value does not erase its group exclusions or occupied slot. A cleanup annul may remove a wanted modifier instead; a side reforge may destroy all wanted modifiers on that side; a whole-item reforge may preserve non-affix tier context; crafted or fractured identity may change the available cleanup actions.

Consequently there is no general implication

`non-goal modifier ⇒ free to ignore ⇒ equivalent clean carrier`.

The safe relaxation of the complete-held clean-side gate is: allow a dirty or incomplete entry only when its exact word law and all paid cleanup/recovery exits are represented, and the whole controller is checked. This is a larger proposal domain, not approximate state merging or a changed goal predicate.

A further synthetic counterexample illustrates why a removal cost alone is insufficient. A carrier contains one desired modifier and one junk modifier. A uniform annul removes either with probability one half. Charging one annul and assuming the desired modifier survives omits a positive reacquisition obligation. Repeating until “clean” requires the actual state-dependent chain, not a deterministic cleanup surcharge. This illustration is mathematical; native annul distributions for a real item must come from the engine.

### 5.6 Final Exalt and switching finishers

When three distinct held natural affixes occupy all three slots on one side, an admitted ordinary Exalt can add only on the other side. This supplies the directional argument for the private final-fill change. It does not make the added affix a requested goal. Native wrong-tier and junk exits still require continuation. If the other side is not actually full, ordinary Exalt can affect it and invalidate the directional premise. [S04, S07, S14]

An economically sensible switch must compare full continuation cost. Consider this **synthetic arithmetic example**, not PoE data:

- A cheap attempt costs 2, succeeds with probability 0.1, and otherwise enters a tail costing 100. Its value is `2 + 0.9×100 = 92`.
- A dearer attempt costs 20, succeeds with probability 0.9, and otherwise enters a tail costing 10. Its value is `20 + 0.1×10 = 21`.

Immediate word price ranks them backwards. A native temporary blocker may be useful because it changes exit probabilities; its price is only one term. Comparing blocked and plain Exalt must include setup, removal, loss branches and the precise tier/control context of every recovery. The failed historical plain-fill test is a direct warning against treating the two words as interchangeable.

For repeated trials, `c/p` is valid only for genuinely identical trial/reset states and the stated reward model. With attempt cost `c`, failure probability `1-p`, and mandatory identical-state reset cost `d`,

\[
J=c+(1-p)(d+J)
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
J=\frac{c+(1-p)d}{p},\qquad p>0.
\]

If a failed attempt changes a tier, blocker, held goal or resource state, this renewal equation is not the model. Use the transition matrix instead. If `p=0`, no finite retry value exists. [S17]

### 5.7 Why jointly testing two edits is justified

Let `π` be a proper baseline and `μ` a complete proper candidate on a common finite domain. Define the baseline-relative advantage

\[
A_\mu^\pi=c_\mu+Q_\mu J_\pi-J_\pi.
\]

Subtracting their Bellman equations gives

\[
J_\mu-J_\pi
=(I-Q_\mu)^{-1}A_\mu^\pi.
\]

The inverse has nonnegative entries. Therefore nonpositive advantage at every relevant state is sufficient for nonincrease; strictly negative advantage on a state with positive **candidate** root occupancy implies strict root improvement. Baseline occupancy is not the correct general weighting for a recurrently changed policy. This derivation requires compatible tail states and properness; it is not a way to certify an incompletely checked candidate. [S17]

A strict single-edit root-improvement filter can miss complementary choices. The existing policy chapter's example has baseline root → goal cost 10, and an off-policy state `t` → goal cost 100. Adding a root → `t` bridge costing 1 alone makes cost 101. Improving `t` → goal to cost 1 alone leaves root cost 10. Adding both makes root cost 2. Hence a bounded search over **one partial-growth change and one finishing change together** has a principled motivation. It does not justify unlimited grammar growth or prove that these two particular real changes are sufficient.

If a proposed entry does not belong to the checked baseline domain, the advantage equation cannot borrow an unrelated value. The choices are to establish the new compatible continuation with the existing checker, or omit that candidate from the bounded portfolio and report the coverage limitation.

### 5.8 Incumbent monotonicity versus bounded-search performance

For a fixed collection of fully qualified policies, adding another cannot increase the mathematical minimum:

\[
\min_{\pi\in C\cup\{\mu\}}J_\pi(s_0)
\le \min_{\pi\in C}J_\pi(s_0).
\]

But a fixed-time implementation may finish checking a **different collection** after new work is added. Baseline generation or retention can be delayed, memory can be refused, or useful candidates can be starved. The inequality therefore does not prove resource noninterference.

The implementation guarantee should be narrower and executable: once a checked incumbent tuple `(θ, artifact, complete admission evidence, value)` exists, optional construction may not mutate or evict it; failed or incomplete candidates cannot replace it; and replacement requires the unchanged native authority check and an acceptable value comparison. Existing baseline work retains its declared priority and reservation. If there is no remaining optional headroom, skip the extension rather than consume the incumbent's ownership.

For a cold run, preserving the source of the old winning route does not alone guarantee reproducing exactly 101,311.35474896732 before the same deadline. That requires matched execution evidence. This packet proposes neither a saved-graph import masquerading as generation nor a new implicit warm-start assumption. The retained 101k graph remains a comparison artifact; an already-live 101k incumbent remains protected while candidates compete.

### 9.2 Doc-ready mathematical addition

**Proposed text for `policies.md#return-bridges`, adjacent to the existing October construction note:**

> A guarded proposal region is not a native equivalence class. It may select the same controller decision for several items, while each item's exact transition and resource law remains independently represented. Eligibility of a missing goal requires a positive native member under the actual item context and all exclusion groups. Clearing the target side distinguishes removable target blockers from exclusions retained on the other side. Per-goal compatibility after clearing is necessary for a preserving reroll strategy, but does not prove joint attainability of all target goals: two individually eligible goal members may exclude each other.
>
> A generated controller may deliberately carry unmatched affixes while it grows useful progress. Under a clean terminal predicate, those affixes remain cleanup debt. Every removal outcome, loss of a desired affix, setup, temporary craft removal and reacquisition cost belongs to the same original-root controller. Whole-item reacquisition is not return to the original root when persistent tiers or other native context differ.
>
> For a finite intervention followed by a compatible proper continuation, keep intervention phase in the semantic state. If every positive exit has the declared continuation and the intervention has almost-sure finite duration and finite paid reward, the stopped-process equation applies. A bounded attempt counter proves only termination of that intervention. Resetting it on every reacquisition can produce a non-goal recurrent class; the complete emitted controller still requires properness. No root scalar supplies an absent continuation value.
>
> The partial-held native witness establishes a bounded construction omission, not all-search absence or whole-policy recovery. Its newly generated competitors remain candidates until complete original-root evaluation and every required positive programme-entry check pass. This is an application of the existing policy/programme arguments, with no additional lower or exact-closure authority.
