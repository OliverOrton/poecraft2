### 4.3 Complete selected probability, not every alternative's future

For every action promoted into a proposed controller, all positive-mass successors and observed choices require valid continuations. A state with unknown routes is not a terminal or zero-valued upper. Native options retain paid setup, conditional lock removal, rarity recovery, any lost goal, and actual mandatory retry control.

For unselected alternatives, it is permissible to defer their kernels or descendants. Keep their scope obligations and revisitable status; do not label them dominated or close the original proof. A rare failure exit cannot be dropped because it is unlikely.

A small patch may need cooperating actions at several entries. Permit the existing candidate-local continuation to close those dependencies even if the first edit alone looks worse. Do not demand a strict root decrease after every installed row. Conversely, do not expand every alternative of each newly reached tail: select a coherent continuation first, then consider competitors.

### 4.4 Rank opportunities at the level that can change work

Use existing finite private continuation values and native expected-cost calculations to propose a local advantage where the needed row is complete. For an unbuilt row, use a clearly nonauthoritative score from legal effect, remaining goal/side structure, price, occupancy and cheaply available native pool information. Keep expected crafting cost separate from predicted compute effort.

Current occupancy may prioritize frequently revisited opportunities, but old-visits times advantage is not an exact changed-root saving. The mathematical handoff supplies the correct repeated-policy response and a counterexample. Use a small recurrent calculation where it is already available; do not add a general inverse/low-rank backend as a prerequisite.

Unknown or initially pessimistic alternatives retain an ordinary/age path. Reconsider after a relevant value, row, candidate or budget change, not on each host step. Freeze decisions of a candidate being certified even if estimates change. Cheap exact inapplicability checks can avoid unsupported option work, but must reuse native predicates rather than duplicate crafting rules.

The three-family adaptive diagnostic may remain off. Only reconsider learning if a fixed native decision view shows it can rank actual competing opportunities differently and the expected benefit exceeds its cost. No automatic B/S/A rerun or predictor sweep is selected.
