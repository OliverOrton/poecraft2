## Objective and decision rule

Use the good Conquest-four strategies to understand which structural capabilities the expensive Bow-four and Ring-four strategies lack. Separate four causes: rare acquisition, loss-driven reacquisition, missing complete continuation coverage, and representation/checker burden. Then choose the smallest native-owner change supported by an actual comparison.

The product objective remains **minimum expected chaos cost from the original request entry**, with a complete independently evaluated executable policy and honest full-scope proof status. Counts, phase models and rankings diagnose or propose; they do not change prices, grant free items, supply lower bounds or close competitors. [R01, R13–R16]

The first useful deliverable is a causal diagnosis, not a new feature. A successful research phase may show that a suspected alternative is fully verified but more expensive. That is informative. An unsupported kernel, missing tail, invalid observation, resource cap or failed publication is a different outcome and must remain distinguishable.

## Phase 4 — Run discriminating complete-controller experiments

### Experiment order

Start with the fixed incumbent and a bounded entry comparison. Reuse completed rows within their compatible immutable layout. Run only the branches selected by the preceding ledger.

| Experiment | What is held fixed | What changes | Question answered |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recovery/retention contrast | Original entry and compatible acquisition laws | One complete alternative continuation/recovery controller | Is later loss causing acquisition repetition? |
| Acquisition contrast | Original entry and compatible recovery law | One complete alternative acquisition controller | Is a useful departure intrinsically expensive under current acquisition? |
| Entry-selection contrast | Candidate family, work limits and native identities | Single-entry service versus a bounded compatible entry group | Is opportunity fragmented across current entries? |
| Representation contrast | Physical entry and intended supported candidate family | Fresh required observation or justified private observer subset | Is construction failing because of missing/excess distinctions? |
| Paid-anchor contrast | Original root, objective, full native scope and prices | One alternative paid acquisition/fracture/progress order | Is the fixed earned anchor itself a bad economic commitment? |

A common recovery law is a premise, not something to assume: an acquisition change may create exits that the old recovery cannot handle. Complete those tails and classify the additional change, or reject the “one-factor” label. The same caution applies to changing action observers.

Evaluate one-time deviations separately from permanent replacements. A permanent recurring patch changes occupancy, properness and the whole-root value. Do not use old visits times a local delta as the acceptance calculation. [R14]

Start with one hard case and a Conquest continuation contrast; transfer the selected semantic mechanism to the second hard case only after the first result explains something. Do not run every row of the table on every fixture by default.

Where an existing fixture already covers an invariant, reuse it rather than add another equivalent test. The synthetic checks shipped with this research explain the equations but are not substitutes for these native fixtures.

**Suggested practical milestone for this new programme:** at least a 25% verified expected-cost reduction on one hard primary with a semantic explanation and no loss of a cheaper retained policy on the other primary/control. Current reference thresholds are:

- Bow: at most **167,511.7500294858** chaos.
