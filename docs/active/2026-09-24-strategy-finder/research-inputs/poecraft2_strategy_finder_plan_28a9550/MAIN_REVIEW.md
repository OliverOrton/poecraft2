The project has already separated some upper proposals from proof. The proposed change is a **peer search owner with its own candidate representation and budget allocation**, rather than a flag threaded through the large exact scheduler. A reduction in proof overhead is not assumed until measured; some present cost is required selected-policy construction/checking, not optional optimality proof.

## 4. What earlier negative work does and does not imply

Low-level cross-binding templates, same-entry memos, role censuses, memory-lifetime trials and strict-preparation audits failed their selected usefulness gates. They do not establish that role features cannot guide heuristic search. They also do not justify another exact reuse framework, cache, observer engine or broad refactor as a prerequisite.

A heuristic role representation can intentionally alias states. Native bindings and checked policies cannot. This is a different experiment from proving an exact quotient or amortizing an identical arithmetic DAG.

## 5. Why now, with qualifications

The native transition/compiler/evaluator/tooling foundation is substantial enough for a controlled new proposer. Recent outcomes give stronger evidence of a candidate-quality/coverage problem than of a universal missing cache. The user explicitly authorizes evaluating more aggressive discovery.

The strongest remaining risks are (a) constructing complete useful controllers rather than fragments, (b) checking their native state products within budget, (c) compactly expressing conditional choices without a model dependency at execution time, and (d) avoiding training on biased or falsely exact labels. The first milestone addresses these before model training.

This is an architectural recommendation and falsifiable programme, not evidence of a future speedup, optimality result, or publishable novelty.
