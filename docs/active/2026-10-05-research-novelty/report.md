## 4. Prior art and honest novelty boundaries

**Options.** Sutton, Precup and Singh define temporally extended closed-loop actions with initiation, internal policy and termination, and consequence models containing accumulated reward and termination-state predictions. This is prior art for a crafting word's cost/exit interface. Their discounted planning theory is not a substitute for poecraft2's undiscounted proper SSP, native support or paid setup/cleanup. The option concept does not prescribe a curated recipe bank. [L1, §§2–3]

**Local-policy decomposition.** Dean and Lin parameterize interactions between regions and combine local policies through abstract planning or iterative parameter refinement. Their paper already makes downstream interaction values central to selecting a local policy. Region-based composition and tail-sensitive local competition therefore cannot be called new. Choosing a useful native region and preserving actual controller memory remain additional obligations here. [L2, §§1.3, 3–5]

**MAXQ.** Dietterich's value decomposition works within a supplied task hierarchy and gives conditions for safe abstraction; recursive optimality is a hierarchy-relative notion. It is useful as a warning against assigning one universal value to a role-level subtask. A fixed hand-designed hierarchy is a poorer fit to Oliver's cold-start preference, and no hierarchy-relative optimum closes this solver's full permitted programme scope. [L3]

**Exact compositional expected rewards.** Watanabe, Eberhart, Asada and Hasuo explicitly develop expected-reward decomposition for open MDPs, carrying exit reachability along with rewards. Their Remark 2.1 assumes almost-sure termination under every scheduler for their framework; poecraft2 permits improper candidate controllers and must check the selected composition instead. This paper decisively rules out a broad novelty claim for exact expected-reward composition. [L4, §§2.2–3]

**Pareto caching.** Watanabe, van der Vegt, Junges and Hasuo reuse component verification across weighted exit queries. Their CAV 2024 algorithm is approximate weighted-reachability verification with sound error bounds, not an exact paid-SSP endpoint or native crafting implementation. The relationship to a response frontier is close enough that claiming discovery of Pareto reuse would be wrong. The exact bounded list proposed here adopts only the interface/competition idea; it does not import approximate answers as exact values. [L5, §§2.3, 3, 5–6]

**Local Pareto alternatives for unknown contexts.** Watanabe, van der Vegt, Hasuo, Rot and Junges explicitly retain local schedulers offering Pareto-optimal exit tradeoffs because the surrounding context determines which tradeoff is useful. Their TACAS 2024 paper recursively combines Pareto curves and develops sound approximations to avoid an exponential scheduler set. This is direct prior art for retaining response alternatives before their tails are known; it supplies the bottom-up basis discussed in CAV 2024. Its reachability objective and approximate curve implementation do not qualify exact native SSP responses here. [L6, §§1, 4–6]

**Recursive option construction.** Silver and Ciosek's ICML 2012 algorithm constructs and composes option models from lower-level models, optimizing policies and termination against supplied subgoals. Their approach does not require a pre-solved recipe bank; their experiments include an undiscounted episodic Tower of Hanoi. Cold derivation, recursive construction and model composition are therefore established concepts. Subgoal supply, the base model set and hierarchical constraints still matter, and the paper establishes no native crafting admission, graph-only certificate bridge or poecraft2 bounded cost result. [L7, §§1, 4–6]

The classification is **potentially useful adaptation; novelty unestablished**. The narrower empirical question is whether the native graph/entry/scope contract permits a bounded generator to reach a genuinely different complete controller, and whether response comparison reduces all-in work for that same candidate set. ## 6. Mathematics: exact interfaces, a narrow screen and its limits

All propositions in this section concern complete finite mathematical models. Numeric implementation must separately satisfy section 8's coefficient and acceptance contract.

### 6.1 A fixed local controller has an affine tail response

Fix internal states I and external non-goal ports Z; a goal port has continuation zero. Let Q be the nonnegative internal substochastic matrix, H the complete probability matrix to Z, and G the goal probabilities. Assume `Q*1 + H*1 + G*1 = 1`, Q is transient, immediate cost c is finite/nonnegative, and decisions are fixed at their legitimate observation points. Then

\[
v(u)=(I-Q)^{-1}(c+Hu)=g+Tu,
\quad g=(I-Q)^{-1}c,
\quad T=(I-Q)^{-1}H.
\]

Proof: expand the nonnegative convergent Neumann series. The nth term is work or exit mass after n internal steps. Summing over all n gives accumulated cost and first-exit probabilities. Equivalently solve the fixed-policy equations. This is the existing stopped-response argument, not a new theorem. [M1]

For resource k with immediate expected quantity vector `r_k`, use `g_k=(I-Q)^-1 r_k`, not a prepriced aggregate if later price-only reuse is contemplated. Expected primitive executions require their own reward. For branch/resource evidence, define the full exit set `Z_all = Z ∪ Z_goal`, including direct goal exits, and retain for every `z ∈ Z_all`

\[
W_{i,z,k}=\mathbb E_i\left[\sum_{n<\tau}R_{n,k}\;1_{S_\tau=z}\right].
\]

The sum `Σ_{z ∈ Z_all} W_{i,z,k}` equals `g_k(i)`. Summing only over the non-goal boundary Z would omit resources spent on trajectories that finish directly at a goal. Goal continuation value is zero; the resources already paid on a goal-ending trajectory need not be zero. W is an unconditional joint resource mass, not conditional cost at an exit. The native leaf verifier already represents these quantities. Constant-price expected cost needs the total resource expectation; exit-conditioned reporting or further joint composition must not substitute an unconditional average for W. [S4]

No equation here grants a value to an unknown port. Nor does it make two entries equal. Each entry retains a separate response row unless complete native equality has been proved.

### 6.2 A strict dominance screen that does not need tail values

Compare two complete local alternatives a and b at the same exact entry and exactly aligned ports, both transient internally. If

\[
g_a\le g_b,\qquad T_a(z)\le T_b(z)\quad\text{for every non-goal port }z,
\]

then `g_a + T_a u <= g_b + T_b u` for every finite nonnegative tail vector u. Conversely, dominance for every such unconstrained u requires exactly these inequalities. Setting u=0 isolates `g_a<=g_b`. If any coordinate has `T_a(z)>T_b(z)`, choose u zero elsewhere and grow its z coordinate to a sufficiently large finite number; that positive coefficient eventually outweighs the finite cost difference and contradicts dominance. Thus the componentwise conditions are necessary as well as sufficient for this universal affine-response statement. They neither estimate u nor discover a continuation.

For repeated composition, stronger global premises are needed. Contract a complete proper incumbent onto a finite common boundary set B, retaining goal absorption, with expected per-macro cost vector d and substochastic boundary matrix K. Replace only some macro rows with locally complete transient alternatives, retaining unchanged outside routing. Require for every replaced row `d'<=d` and `K'<=K` componentwise; unchanged rows are equal. Then the new contracted controller is proper and no more expensive.

Proof: `0<=K'<=K` implies `(K')^n<=K^n` by induction. Properness of the old finite boundary chain gives `K^n->0`, hence `(K')^n->0`. With finite nonnegative costs, termwise comparison gives

\[
V'=\sum_{n\ge0}(K')^n d'\le\sum_{n\ge0}K^n d=V.
\]

Transient fragment interiors and finite internal costs lift boundary properness to the finite flattened controller. Strict root improvement additionally needs a strict negative advantage at a boundary reached with positive expected visits under the new controller; mere strictness at an unreachable boundary changes no root cost. The published graph still goes through independent native evaluation.

This route requires no scalar continuation transfer, but it requires **complete aligned native boundary laws** and a proper incumbent composition. A checked root artifact alone does not supply those laws automatically. It can be the executable object from which the old response is constructed. If a new alternative reaches an off-incumbent port with positive mass, `K'<=K` fails at that port; no old scalar fills the gap.

The universal condition is restrictive rather than merely a loose sufficient heuristic. If neither controller has direct goal exits and both complete non-goal first-exit distributions sum to one, componentwise dominance forces identical exit laws: nonnegative component differences sum to zero. Meaningful universal domination then comes from lower paid cost under equal exits, or from shifting non-goal mass to verified goal absorption. Growth/reroll alternatives that trade one dirty exit against another are generally incomparable. Removing a redundant paid Annul before an erasing reroll could be eligible only after native whole-kernel/routing equality; that concrete question is already in P4, not newly discovered here.

### 6.3 A frontier retains tail-sensitive alternatives

For a finite generated set O of complete local controllers, at a fixed known compatible tail vector u the best entry response is

\[
\phi_i(u)=\min_{o\in O}\{g_{o,i}+T_{o,i}u\}.
\]

This is the lower envelope of finitely many affine **policy costs**, relative to O. It is not a lower bound on the unrestricted crafting optimum, and not an exact closure claim. Choosing one controller at a genuinely observable entry is executable; independently taking minima at hidden internal states is a different controller and needs fresh rows and checking.

Illustration: two alternatives exit to a non-goal tail costing u. A costs 1 and exits there with probability 0.9; B costs 4 and exits there with probability 0.2; the other mass is verified goal. Their one-shot responses cross at `u=30/7`. At u=2, A costs 2.8 and B 4.4; at u=20, A costs 19 and B 8. No common role signature or cheapest-internal-cost rule chooses correctly across these contexts. This is a hand-derived finite example, not a PoE law.

If every port value has a legitimate finite interval `L_z<=u_z<=U_z`, a box suffices to prove a limited dominance statement. With `delta=g_a-g_b` and `w=T_a-T_b`,

\[
\max_{u\in[L,U]}(\delta+wu)
=\delta+\sum_{w_z\ge0}w_zU_z+\sum_{w_z<0}w_zL_z.
\]

A nonpositive result establishes dominance on that box only. If an upper is unknown/infinite and the corresponding w is positive, the screen cannot pass. TargetNeutralZero supplies the allowed global lower zero under its nonnegative-cost premises; it does not supply finite upper values at arbitrary ports. An approximate copied table with sticky rejection cannot define this box. Properness of the selected recurring composition remains separate unless the strict matrix-dominance argument applies.

Stronger screening therefore requires a **justified constrained tail domain**, not a better guess at arbitrary nonnegative values. Examples could be independently checked finite entry uppers with compatible lower endpoints, proved equality between two port continuations, or mutually consistent boundary equations for a specified complete outer controller. Each constraint needs native entry/control/scope and coefficient evidence. A sampled cost, a role relationship, one root scalar at a different port, or the fact that a candidate generator considered a tail supplies no such constraint. If no valid domain is available, retain the incomparable alternatives within the proposal bound or evaluate complete graphs; do not imply that an unconstrained response frontier will prune them.

### 6.4 Feedback must be solved, not priced as fixed old tails

If a fragment returns to its own entry with probability q and costs g per complete excursion, its repeated controller costs `g/(1-q)` for q<1, including every paid recovery in g. At q=1 it does not reach goal; a zero-cost loop may satisfy a Bellman equation yet is improper.

For several recurrent ports, assemble the actual selected boundary matrix K and reward d, then solve `V_B=d+K V_B` only after proving K transient. Eliminating transient interior states is a Schur complement; the repository already gives its exact form. Local exit with probability one does not prevent two components from alternating forever. [M1]

One-shot scores against the old root value do not rank repeated policies. With old cost 10, a complete excursion having goal probability 0.9 and repeated cost 8 has one-shot response 8.2; an excursion having goal probability 0.01 and repeated cost 1 has one-shot response 9.91. The first scores better once, but the second is cheaper when repeated. Those figures follow from `(g,q)=(7.2,0.1)` and `(0.01,0.99)`. This known counterexample is already recorded in policy mathematics.

The deterministic bridge/tail example is equally relevant: incumbent `r->goal` costs 10, `t->goal` costs 100; alternatives `r->t` cost 1 and `t->goal` cost 1. Bridge alone costs 101, tail alone leaves root cost 10, joint changes cost 2. A frontier may retain ingredients for a joint controller, but it must carry the real t continuation and check the assembled graph. Existing full-domain policy iteration need not have this bounded-proposal failure. [M1, P1, P2]

### 6.5 Counterexamples that delimit the proposal

| Invalid shortcut | Counterexample or failing premise |
|---|---|
| Equal goal masks imply equal ports | Two same-mask items differ in below-tier member, secondary group exclusion, crafted blocker or persistent tier; next action legality/law changes. |
| An exact leaf proves an exact class | One leaf is valid, another member has no legal cleanup. The first leaf covers neither the second member nor an executable common selector. |
| Proper fragments imply proper composition | One exits from x to y, another from y to x; no goal ever reached. |
| Drop a tiny-positive recovery/trap exit | A positive trap, however small, destroys almost-sure goal completion; renormalizing success changes the problem. |
| Borrow the root cost at a dirty port | Same visible goals but different item/controller phase has a different continuation; the root-entry certificate does not apply. |
| Dominance at the sampled representative | A second member's exit probability reverses the inequality; universal domain comparison was absent. |
| Cache a best decision after price change | The fixed response may remain valid, but the selected minimum and old paid cost change. |
| Small residual certifies dominance | Near q=1, value error is residual divided by `1-q`; an uncertain comparison can reverse. |
| Matching descriptor digest is equality | A digest locates candidates; canonical fields and native equality still establish the relation. |
| Retry after free repair | A reset returns a similar root while retaining different persistent context, or omits paid acquisition; the renewal equation is for another controller. |

### Cache-free first

The initial batch owns its few complete responses, exact port maps and fixed controllers until selection/checking or refusal, then releases them in dependency order. There is no cross-request solved policy bank. This gives a cold-start comparison and limits invalidation to ordinary request-local compatibility checks. Same-batch deduplication by exact complete law remains allowed, but it must be compared against deduplication the current native owner already performs.

A cached second version uses an **exact semantic dependency key**, not a role-name signature. It can reuse a fixed controller's immutable transition/resource response only when its entry member domain, mechanics, programme scope, goal/terminal, control observations, action vocabulary, row/port map and numerical semantics agree. Use canonical dependency fields/native equality after hash lookup. Expanding the key to include every dependency may make hits rare; that is an empirical result to report, not a reason to coarsen the key. Exact-cache invalidation costs include key construction, lookup, deep equality, version migration, certificate reattachment, admission, retained memory, eviction/destruction and recomputation charges. [M2–M4, S4, S6]

Let C be uncached response construction cost, L lookup/validation cost, H extra retention/eviction cost over m requests, and R full cold work still required per hit. A first-build plus m-1 exact hits beats m cold constructions only if

\[
(m-1)(C-R)>mL+H.
\]

All terms are charged to the actual native owner, and the original-root outcome is still the acceptance metric. The cache does not help a unique cold request merely by having a high hit rate in a prewarmed benchmark. A paired comparison must disclose initialization, warm history and all failures; saved graphs are not independent trials.

### Complexity and the actual bottleneck hypothesis

Let n be internal product states, e positive transitions, z exits, k resource channels and f retained local alternatives. Complete native expansion costs at least the existing kernel/materialization work plus O(n+e) traversal/SCC work. A dense fixed-policy factorization is O(n^3) time/O(n^2) memory, then approximately O(n^2(z+k)) for multiple right-hand sides. Sparse cost depends on fill and conditioning and can approach dense cost; it must be measured, not described as automatically linear. Retaining joint exit-resource responses can add O(nzk) work/storage unless a justified structure reduces it.

At one exact entry, pairwise strict dominance on already materialized response rows costs O(f^2 z), plus resource pricing. Multi-entry coverage costs multiply by the actual admitted entry domain. Computing an exact complete frontier over all local policies can require exponentially many alternatives; this proposal uses a bounded generated set and claims only its best checked feasible output. It never enumerates a full Pareto set in production by default.

The flattened checker has its own operation/item product, global observation union, entry census and programme admission work. A smaller response summary does not automatically reduce that checker carrier or peak. Similarly, a saved frontier can add memory overlap while the old fallback, generation context and checker are simultaneously alive. The existing aggregate 1 GiB limit is retained; no new separate response budget multiplies parent headroom.

Thus the most plausible benefit is **fewer full candidate checks or fewer repeated tail-sensitive selections after a complete response is already available**. The least supported benefit is acceleration of primitive native kernel construction or universal state compression. This distinction is the new materiality premise and must be measured before implementation broadens.

### 16.1 Priority 1 — what finite grammar completeness can mean

**Observed source and receipt.** `research.md#gap-03` explicitly leaves caller scope, primitive/dependency actions, delayed families, mandatory programmes, restore memory, decision timing and complete member coverage open. `claims.md#clm-0004` records a scoped earlier bounded complete-held gate observation and refuses an all-search absence conclusion. The finite native receipt is at source `a9aadac6c5d2905ee3cd832290dd4fef53c5e893`: 416 checks, 35 certified physical entries (root, P0 and 33 paid-word exits), complete positive exit mass one, native word cost 3.7463, and word plus fixed checked Current tails 101314.94475391766. Its scope is explicitly “one-use native word followed by fixed checked Current tails; exact physical entries only.” It loses 3.59000495013606 at P0. No receipt turns those 35 members into coverage of all legal members of an unnamed controller class. [E4 below, M0, M1]

A completeness certificate needs a **finite declared class**, not a finite test count. Define a target identity theta containing native data/mechanics/laws, original root, goal, prices, action/programme scope, observable predicates, checkpoint/offer memory and compiler/evaluator semantics. Separately define a finite grammar `G_M`: allowed native descriptors and finite parameter domains, permitted observation/guard alphabet O, at most M controller-memory nodes, and explicit mandatory-program templates. Loop execution may be unbounded while the controller's description remains finite; a recursive grammar with unbounded description size is not finite merely because its primitives are finite. Exclude unsupported numeric observations and unmodeled native context from this class explicitly, rather than pretending they have been covered.

Let C(theta, G_M, D) be the deterministic controllers described by this grammar on the specified admitted physical entry/member domain D. Randomized controller choices, larger memory or additional observations are outside C unless expressly included. A grammar manifest must state which native action families and mandatory subprogrammes it expands, which are dependency-only, which are delayed, and which are unsupported. Each generated programme needs a derivation and descriptor/control identity; a legal primitive catalogue is not programme enumeration.

There are three separate completeness questions:

1. **Syntactic enumeration:** every admissible description in G_M is generated, represented by a proved equivalent description, or disposed of by a valid exclusion witness.
2. **Native correspondence:** each generated controller implements the claimed paid actions, actual observation timing and control memory on every member of its admitted D; all strictly positive successors are represented or have complete compatible tails. A representative physical item does not prove this universally.
3. **Economic exhaustion within C:** every proper candidate in C is evaluated or soundly excluded as unable to beat the retained candidate under a justified comparison. This is relative optimality within C only, and needs a legitimate numerical issuer/comparison, not a tiny residual or approximate ranking.

A scoped certificate can prove question 1 without proving 2 or 3. Conversely, independently checking one emitted graph establishes a feasible witness without proving enumeration completeness. C may deliberately exclude useful controllers; its complete enumeration then tells us nothing about unrestricted optimality.

**Derived narrow claim.** Assume finite C; complete native correspondence for all admitted members; independent properness and finite paid-cost evidence for accepted controllers; an exhaustive, disjoint coverage manifest; and sound exclusions relative to the same target and admitted candidate domain. If every element of C has an accepted complete controller representative or a valid exclusion, the minimum of the retained evaluated representatives is optimal within the proper subset of C. Proof: map an arbitrary proper member of C to its manifest case. Equivalence preserves the relevant cost; exclusion supplies an admitted no-better witness. Hence none beats the retained minimum. The opposite inequality holds because the retained minimum is itself a member of C. There is no conclusion for a controller outside C, and an incomplete coverage frontier breaks the first implication.

This is ordinary bounded exhaustive search plus correctness certificates. Alur et al.'s *Syntax-Guided Synthesis* already separates a grammar-constrained implementation set from its universally quantified semantic specification and uses enumeration or symbolic search. Necula's proof-carrying-code work supplies prior art for a producer delivering evidence to a smaller checker. Neither establishes this repository's native programme/member correspondence or a profitable controller-search implementation. The useful adaptation is a **small auditable coverage contract at the existing producer boundary**, not a new completeness principle. [L8, L9]

**Two alternatives.** An explicit enumeration manifest stores canonical derivations and dispositions. It is easiest to falsify and inspect, but controller counts grow rapidly: for M memory states, O observations and A actions, even table-based controllers admit up to `M * A^(M*|O|) * M^(M*|O|^2)` descriptions before grammar restrictions, if actions depend on current observation and memory updates on current/next observations. This illustrative upper bound is not a native graph-count measurement. Checking positive native support can dwarf descriptor enumeration.

A symbolic coverage certificate partitions derivations/physical domains and proves a total case split inductively over the grammar, retaining exclusions and unresolved cells. It may avoid listing every description but requires a trusted interpretation of guards, complete physical-membership proofs, safe successor remapping and numerical/exclusion obligations. The G0/D1/S0 results warn that cheap syntactic repetition may conceal costly matching. A hash or row signature cannot replace the proof. No symbolic coverage system is qualified here.

### 16.6 Positive-support certification: structural properness is not cost certification

For a finite fixed controller with complete stochastic rows, true absorbing goals and correct operation/item/control product states, almost-sure goal absorption from a root is equivalent to no reachable bottom strongly connected component being wholly non-goal. Equivalently every root-reachable non-goal state has a positive-support path to a goal. Proof: a finite chain eventually reaches a bottom component with probability one; a non-goal bottom component prevents absorption, while a positive path out contradicts bottom closure. Complete positive support is essential. The existing proper-seed argument constructs a rank witness for a supplied finite action view; it already states that unbuilt alternatives and unproved abstraction remain outside its completeness claim. [M1]

Finite properness with finite per-step costs gives finite expected spend. One can choose a finite path-length bound m and minimum positive path probability epsilon over reachable states; blocks of m steps escape with probability at least epsilon, so `E[tau]≤m/epsilon`. This proves finiteness, not cheap evaluation or practical latency: epsilon may be tiny. If support is unchanged under a coefficient change, a fixed graph's properness can survive; its expected spend, numerical certificate and action ranking do not follow.

A complete policy/native certificate must still establish each reachable physical member, native legality, full row/offer routing, mandatory paid setup/retry/recovery, exact goal predicate, compiler route and independent accepted original-root evaluation. [L13]

Falsify a claimed support certificate by adding an epsilon-probability trap, an unsupported positive offer, or a missing physical member; it fails even if all sampled paths reach goal. Falsify a cost certificate with equal support but unequal probabilities or paid rewards. This is an existing required verification boundary with possible certificate transport engineering; no novel support theorem or weakening of native checks is proposed.

### 16.7 Optional-service noninterference under shared budgets

Let U_B(T,M) be the best compatible complete checked policy found by baseline B within a fixed time T and memory M, and U_(B+H)(T,M) the result when optional H shares those resources. Within the augmented run, retaining the pre-H incumbent U_0 guarantees its final winner is no worse than U_0, subject to compatible identity and proper ownership. A successful independently admitted H candidate gives `U_after≤min(U_0,U_H)`. A failed H adds no feasible candidate. None of these statements proves equality with the counterfactual baseline's later winner.

Counterexample: the baseline needs two work units to improve incumbent 20 to 10 under a two-unit budget. Failed H consumes one unit; the baseline cannot complete its improvement, so augmented output stays 20. Thus `U_withoutH=U_withFailedH` is false. If H instead consumes two units and succeeds at 15, it improves the retained 20 while the H-free baseline still reaches 10. Even `U_withSuccessfulH≤U_withoutH` is false under the same shared budget. Peak-memory retention can similarly prevent baseline work that otherwise fits.

A sufficient counterfactual theorem requires additional premises: identical baseline state/input, identical baseline work sequence and completed obligations, no eviction/invalidation of baseline witnesses, and at least the same baseline resource opportunity. At an identical baseline-work checkpoint, adding an independently accepted H portfolio and retaining baseline evidence yields `U_augmented≤U_baseline`, with equality if H adds no better candidate. Achieving that checkpoint may take longer or need reserved additional capacity; it must not be sold as a same-T/M guarantee.

The product obligation is narrower: H failure/cancellation cannot corrupt committed baseline evidence, leak its owner, leave baseline assembly suppressed after release, spend beyond the parent allowance or forge an upper. Scheduling opportunity cost remains measured. A useful paired comparison holds total budget fixed and records baseline work displaced, time to first checked policy, retained best root cost, refusals, maximum owned bytes and actual release. It may show improvement, equality or regression. Standard minimum-over-retained-candidates monotonicity already proves the within-run claim; no stronger noninterference novelty is established.

### 17.2 Derived invariance theorem and conservative invalidation rule

Work with two finite fixed-controller semantic chains, old and patched, over a common named augmented-state domain after a verified embedding. Augmented states contain every item field and controller variable that can influence legality, routing, observation, checkpoint/offer behavior, retry, resource expenditure or termination. This embedding is an explicit obligation when a patch introduces states or renames compiled nodes. Use Q and Q' for non-goal transition matrices, c and c' for finite nonnegative priced immediate rewards, and fixed true goal semantics. Every row is complete, native-admitted and stochastic when its goal exits are included. The statements below are about an executable fixed policy, not an MDP optimum or lower bound.

**Theorem A — closed unchanged continuation.** Let U be a set closed under every positive non-goal successor in both controllers. Suppose all rows, immediate rewards, terminal classifications and augmented-state identities in U are identical under the embedding, and the old controller is proper from each certified entry in U. Then J'(u) = J(u) for every such u, independently of either controller's upstream initial distribution or arrival frequency.

**Proof sketch.** U cannot leave to an altered non-goal state; only true goals are outside its non-goal submatrix. Thus Q'_UU = Q_UU and c'_U = c_U. In a finite proper chain, Q_UU is transient, so the unique finite solution is J_U = (I - Q_UU)^(-1)c_U in both controllers. Equivalently, every conditional path law after entry u and its paid reward is identical. Changed upstream mass multiplies this unchanged conditional value only when computing upstream expectations. The proof requires properness from the requested u; old-root properness does not cover an old-root-unreachable trap. The matrix inverse denotes a solve and a mathematical identity, not an instruction to store a dense inverse.

**Theorem B — sufficient dependency invalidation.** Form the union of the old and new positive non-goal support graphs on the verified common domain. Let D contain every changed transition, priced reward, resource law, terminal classification, routing or selected-policy row, all new/unmatched states, and every state whose authority binding changed. Let A be D together with all states that can reach D in this union graph. An old certificate whose complete continuation domain lies in U = N \ A may be reused under Theorem A, subject to its other native, ownership and numerical bindings.

**Proof sketch.** A is backward closed. A state in U cannot have an old or new positive edge into A, or it could reach D and belong to A. U is therefore closed, its old/new rows match, and Theorem A applies on the certified proper domain. Union support handles additions and removals conservatively; a deleted old path can mark extra states dirty but cannot falsely keep an affected state clean. A may include unchanged states whose values happen numerically to cancel. That conservative loss of reuse is acceptable. Discovering D completely is essential: failing to detect an affected router, item member or terminal predicate defeats the theorem.

This rule does not say that only lexically earlier nodes become dirty. It says that every semantic predecessor of a changed dependency becomes dirty. If e can return to a patched node drawn upstream of it, e is itself a predecessor and cannot keep its old scalar value. Within an SCC, every state can reach every other; one changed dependency makes the whole affected SCC dirty. The acyclic object is the **condensation of the complete positive support graph**, not the ordinary strategy graph and not an arbitrary list of certificate entries.

An implementable conservative first version can rebuild the union SCC partition and reverse dependency index for the bounded candidate pair instead of implementing a dynamic SCC algorithm. This costs O(n + m) time and storage for n semantic states and m positive edges, plus full identity/row comparison and native discovery. Added edges can merge old SCCs; old component IDs and topological order must not be retained blindly. The union partition can over-merge components that are separate in each individual graph, which is safe but can erase savings. Recomputing the patched support SCCs and using union reachability only for invalidation is another exact alternative with its own cost.

**Root calculation.** For an upstream region stopped on first entry to clean ports or the true goal, retain its complete expected paid cost g and first-exit probabilities H. Then J_parent = g + H J_ports. Do not use unrestricted visits times continuation value as additive spending: downstream entries overlap. If dirty blocks can return to each other, first eliminate only transient interiors and solve the complete dirty boundary system u = d + K u. It must be proper, with all exits present. An existing conditional J(e) may include returns to a dirty block; such an e is then dirty under Theorem B, even if a tempting drawing labels it downstream.

### 17.3 Reuse has three levels; feedback belongs in an SCC block

For a fixed block B with interior transition Q_BB and outgoing transition Q_BO to actual executable ports O, suppose its interior is transient and all positive exits are known. Define

\[
A_B = I-Q_{BB},\qquad g_B=A_B^{-1}c_B,\qquad H_B=A_B^{-1}Q_{BO},\qquad J_B=g_B+H_BJ_O.
\]

The distinctions are operationally important:

| Retained object | Exact condition for proposed reuse | What must still change or be checked |
|---|---|---|
| Conditional scalar/vector J_B | Identical full downstream law, reward, goal and authority domain, or another accepted proof of equal solution | Upstream weights, root reachability/admission and global properness after relinking |
| First-exit response g_B, H_B | Identical block interior law, complete port identities, selected decisions and priced reward; proper local exit | Recombine changed port values; solve affected feedback and numerical acceptance |
| Numerical factorization/operator for A_B | Identical coefficient matrix and ordering, compatible numerical solver/version/error contract | New right-hand-side solve; renewed residual/acceptance; resource ownership and release |

An unchanged block interior can reuse its response when J_O changes, while its old J_B becomes invalid. An unchanged A_B can reuse factorization when c_B or boundary right-hand sides change, while g_B or H_B may require new solves. Port renaming requires a verified permutation, not an assumed string match. Reusing a factorization after Q_BB changes is a different update algorithm, with conditioning and acceptance obligations; it is not part of the initial proposal.

Factorization reuse for multiple right-hand sides is standard linear algebra. The official LAPACK DGETRS contract solves A X = B using a previously computed DGETRF factorization. This establishes prior art for the computational operation, not the availability of a retained factorization in this engine or a certificate of native cost. [L17](https://www.netlib.org/lapack/explore-html/df/d36/group__getrs_gaacd7a8465c8cc0e4e8b88ba4b453630c.html) Current canonical policy math also records one-row and multi-row response identities; this report does not relabel those identities as new algorithms.

For price-independent reuse, let b_B^k be the immediate count/reward for each primitive resource k and retain R_B^k = A_B^(-1)b_B^k. At fixed prices w, g_B = sum_k w_k R_B^k, with any fixed fees included as additional reward coordinates. Law/support and resource responses can survive a price-only change only when prices do not change this fixed controller's selected decisions or native mechanics. Its old scalar cost certificate cannot. A search policy chosen using the old prices must be reevaluated; equality of transition probabilities does not preserve its economic ranking. A goal exit has zero continuation value but still contributes its complete paid reward. As §6 requires, reward responses include goal-ending trajectories and every failure/setup/cleanup action; there is no subtraction of successful-path cost.

Local transience is insufficient for the linked controller. Two blocks A and B can each terminate their local macro in one step, A returning to B and B returning to A with probability one. Each H row is complete and each interior solve finite, but the linked non-goal chain never reaches the goal. The root candidate must still establish absence of a reachable non-goal bottom SCC, or an equivalent sound transience witness, on the newly linked positive support. In a finite complete fixed chain, this support proof is enough for almost-sure goal reachability and finite expected visits; it says nothing by itself about the correct cost coefficients. No positive edge may be discarded because its probability is small.

### 17.4 Counterexamples that the patch validator must distinguish

All examples in this subsection are hand-derived finite chains, not engine executions.

1. **Feedback into an upstream patch.** At e, pay 1 and move to a. At a, pay 1 and return to e with probability q, otherwise reach the true goal. Then J(e) = 1 + J(a), J(a) = 1 + q J(e), and J(e) = 2/(1-q). Changing the row at a from q = 0.01 to q = 0.60 changes J(e) from 200/99 to 5. The e row is unchanged, yet e reaches the patch and its continuation is not unchanged. The SCC is the correct dependency unit.
2. **Same coarse name, different member mixture.** A coarse entry E contains full members e1 and e2 with certified conditional costs 1 and 100. Arrival mixture (0.99, 0.01) gives 1.99; mixture (0.40, 0.60) gives 60.4. Both individual certificates can remain valid, but the averaged coarse scalar cannot. Uniform member bounds remain bounds only with their declared all-member domain and numerical semantics; they are not exact mixture values.
3. **New positive entry outside the old census.** The old root reaches the goal directly and does not reach a non-goal self-loop t. A patch gives t probability 0.001. Old-root properness and its positive-entry census are insufficient: the patched root is improper. A new goal-reaching e outside the old census is less dramatic but still needs new native admission and a compatible continuation certificate. Neither absence from the old census nor a saved finite table is evidence about it.
4. **Hidden memory.** Two arrivals have the same visible item and compiled node, but one has an active checkpoint, offer, retry counter or remaining budget that alters the next action or price. They are different semantic entries. If a resource limit changes behavior it belongs in augmented identity, not just in a report label. An incoming-path-dependent controller must encode enough memory to make the continuation Markov; otherwise Theorem A's premise is false.
5. **Changing only prices.** A one-action entry consumes two units of a primitive. A unit price change from 3 to 5 leaves its law and resource certificate intact but changes J from 6 to 10. Reusing the old scalar while rebinding the new economy is unsound. Re-scalarizing a verified unpriced resource response is a proposal only if that response actually exists.
6. **Small residual under long retry.** For a self-loop probability 1-p and paid cost 1, J = 1/p. Residual magnitude epsilon can produce value error epsilon/p. At p = 10^(-6), residual 10^(-6) can hide an error of 1. A local tolerance and unchanged dependency hash do not establish an outward upper enclosure after recombination; properness and sensitivity are separate obligations.

These are falsification obligations, not optional corner cases. Every positive native physical member must be represented in the dependency unit, by explicit exact entries or a proved correspondence covering the complete member domain. A class relation must preserve legality, transition pushforward, all paid resource laws, routing, observations, terminal semantics and the relevant continuation relation. Equal goal masks, node text or apparent affix counts are insufficient. For a fresh positive domain D_new, admission must cover D_old union D_new as actually reached after patching, with failures/caps left unresolved. Old-root support alone is not that coverage certificate.

### 17.6 Numerical acceptance, root properness and price/resource separation

The exact mathematical reuse proofs concern the native coefficient model under explicit identity premises. Current exported fields are floating-point values, probabilities and residuals with existing acceptance gates; the source does not make every exported decimal a rigorously outward-rounded native rational interval. The canonical numerical chapter distinguishes this precisely. Reuse must preserve the existing declared contract or introduce and qualify a new enclosure producer; it may not silently reinterpret a small residual as a proof of an exact upper. [M6](https://github.com/OliverOrton/poecraft2/blob/7252027c80856628ed16734583bfc9d6e166458b/docs/solver/mathematics/numerical-closure.md#residual)

One sufficient abstract enclosure construction illustrates the missing obligation. Given complete fixed-policy coefficients Q, a sound finite witness w >= 1 + Qw, w >= 0, implies transience and (I-Q)^(-1)1 <= w. If the accepted candidate v has a sound componentwise residual bound |c + Qv - v| <= epsilon, then v - epsilon w <= J <= v + epsilon w. All coefficient conversion and the inequality checks themselves need justified directions. An exact binary check certifies the represented binary model, not automatically the native integer-weight law. A port interval [l,u] propagates through an exact nonnegative response as [g + Hl, g + Hu]; uncertain g/H need outward error treatment too. Feedback amplifies error according to the proper linked system's transience witness, so bounds must be recomputed or soundly reused there.

This sufficient witness and residual argument is already in M6; it is included as an acceptance obligation, not proposed as a novel issuer. Selecting it at runtime would be separate future numerical work. If only the present tolerance/reconciliation contract is available, report the same numerical estimate semantics as the full evaluator and preserve its gates. No new pruning, lower authority or native-exact claim follows. Qualification must compare full entry values and acceptance disposition, not only close-looking root decimals.

A certificate dependency model should separate at least four layers:

- **Native law/support:** mechanics/count-law, item/member domain, legal selected operations, routes, hidden memory and terminal predicate. A native-law change invalidates associated law, resource and scalar evidence.
- **Unpriced resource response:** every paid primitive, fee, retry, cleanup and goal-ending trajectory under a fixed law. A mechanics/resource-accounting change invalidates this even if probabilities appear equal.
- **Price scalarization:** current frozen economy applied to those responses. A price-only change invalidates scalar costs and any price-dependent policy choice; it is not a data/price refresh authorization.
- **Root admission/properness/occupancy:** current reached full-entry census, linked support and initial distribution. A new incoming probability changes occupancy; newly positive entries change admission obligations. Even where existing law and conditional costs survive, root publication remains a newly checked composition.

Full-root re-evaluation could eventually be replaced by a sound compositional root check, but not by omitting root checks. The check can reuse accepted complete local law/response/properness evidence only under the proved contracts, and must verify the linked system, all new positive domains, every paid tail, current prices and final emitted graph correspondence. Until this producer and consumer are qualified, the ordinary full check remains the authority and incremental results are shadow evidence. The 33 refused upper requests, zero joint attempts and retained root-only evidence in §3 do not qualify this proposed producer.

### 17.7 Cache-free versus exact versioned cache and costs

**Alternative A — cache-free per-batch linking.** Within a bounded batch of two related candidates, build complete response/dependency records from a currently admitted complete graph, compare its patch against the other graph, reuse compatible units, and discard them with the batch. This cold derivation uses current native objects and avoids a solved recipe bank. It still pays the initial certificate, full-domain comparison, reverse index, SCC construction and extra ownership costs. One candidate or a patch spanning the giant SCC may yield no reuse.

**Alternative B — exact versioned cache.** Persist compatible law/resource/response records with full semantic keys and context/version payloads, verify those payloads on load, recompute reverse invalidation after every patch, and regenerate scalarization/root checks as required. It can amortize repeated exact structures but adds serialization, lookup, collision checking, loading, size caps, lifetime/eviction, stale-version detection and invalidation costs. A code change is not always semantically relevant, but the first implementation should conservatively invalidate the declared relevant engine/schema/compiler/numerical versions. More selective cross-version compatibility needs its own proof. Stored Library/report receipts or old policies are not automatically cache entries. Price-independent records must actually retain unpriced rewards, which the inspected selected-kernel export currently does not.

No approximate-neighbor hits or warm imported policies may masquerade as exact reuse. A cache miss invokes the current full path; a failed or incomplete cache record supplies no authority. Persisted evidence must have a clearly owned, bounded artifact, full private-context compatibility and release behavior. This optional cache is only worth selecting after Alternative A exhibits exact repetition and positive all-in savings. The user's preference for generic cold derivation is therefore consistent with first testing A; B is an evidence-based later option, not the design premise.

For n full augmented states, m positive edges, b retained boundary ports and k resource coordinates, cold support/dependency storage is at least O(n+m), plus full keys/native materializations. A dense stored response can cost O(nb+nk); exposing many physical ports can dominate the purported saving. Dense SCC factorization has cubic arithmetic and quadratic storage in its component size; sparse cost depends on fill, conditioning, solve method and retained workspace rather than only n and m. Multiple right-hand sides add solve cost even when factorization is reused. Exact rational variants can incur coefficient bit growth; they are not free numerical replacements. Repeated SCC mapping/native matching can dominate a small avoided solve, while new positive domain discovery can traverse the whole relevant graph. No complexity expression promises a current wall-time gain.

Define the net time saving against the same complete candidates as

\[
S = T_{full} - (T_{cert}+T_{match}+T_{invalidate}+T_{new-domain}+T_{dirty-solve}+T_{link}+T_{root-check}+T_{release}+T_{cache}).
\]

Report both cold and amortized values, owned peak bytes and actual work displacement under the same limits. Charge source discovery/graph materialization when it is additional, and do not double-count work already required by both arms. Reused entry counts alone are inadequate: many cheap entries can coexist with one dirty expensive SCC. Conversely, a safe old scalar may avoid a numerical solve but not native matching or root admission. Existing G0/ordering/memo negatives and V3's retained row efficiency are the baseline; an abstract DAG is not evidence of more economical search.

### 17.8 Minimal unrun comparison, kill conditions and milestones

**Proposal only:** later select at most two existing complete policy graphs and at most four localized variants already expressible through the ordinary compiler/graph patch interface. Hold candidate bytes, goal, frozen mechanics/prices, native member admission, selected-policy semantics, resource limits and numerical contract identical between arms. Use the current complete full evaluator as arm F and proposed incremental linking as shadow arm I. No new Conquest solve, Simulator campaign, increased memory allowance or builder expansion is implied. This task ran neither arm.

The semantic controls must include an upstream weight-only change with a clean complete downstream entry; a return-to-patched-node/affected-SCC case; a newly positive full-entry domain case; and a price-only scalarization case where a genuinely available resource response is distinguished from a priced-only record. If these controls are not realizable in the two chosen existing graphs, declare the missing premise and use a separately selected small complete model for that obligation. Do not manufacture a native receipt from the hand examples. A losing already constructed graft can still measure verification reuse, but supplies no search improvement; every artifact must be admitted at the selected current context. The historical 85970.67 graph and similarly named old sources are not assumed admitted.

Before any execution, manifest the graph source, exact compiled bytes, full entry/certificate bindings, relevant old/new positive row/member domains and patch set. Predict the conservative invalidation cone from source semantics. Do not use a measured F result to silently alter I's candidate or matching policy. During later qualification, both arms remain necessary because F is the oracle; label that extra qualification cost separately from the prospective production comparison and do not claim that the dual-run pilot itself saves CPU.

| Required observable | Why it is needed |
|---|---|
| Total full entries; reused conditional entries; reused response blocks; new/refused/capped entries | Distinguishes reuse from missing native admission and scalar from response reuse |
| Old/new/union SCC counts, largest sizes; invalidated SCCs/states/rows; reverse-cone size | Shows whether patch locality survives feedback and support changes |
| Native rediscovery and matching work; identity bytes compared; dependency-index build/update cost | Exposes the proof and lookup costs hidden by an entry reuse count |
| Numerical work recomputed, factorization or iterative work actually reused, residual/reconciliation/enclosure disposition | Demonstrates avoided computation under the declared contract; no invented factorization counter |

| Full evaluator versus incremental entry/root values and success/failure disposition | Falsifies unsound reuse even when root decimals accidentally coincide |

Stop the interface direction if a semantic control disagrees, a positive member/price/route is omitted, an incompatible full context survives, an internal-cycle refusal is bypassed, numerical acceptance weakens, or retained memory impairs ordinary work. Stop the performance direction if certificate/matching/dependency costs exceed the optimistic maximum avoided numerical work, the relevant patches invalidate essentially the whole costly SCC, or all-in savings are nonpositive under the bounded comparison. A result that reuses many entries but saves no useful work is a scoped negative. If cold construction independently creates a better complete controller while linking loses, keep that generator result and discard this interface hypothesis.

Bounded milestones, each with an explicit stop before the next:

1. **Source contract audit:** document current graph equality, native identity, entry/kernel refusal, SCC/numerical and ownership dependencies. Identify the minimal supported augmented entry and complete patch delta; no producer activation.
2. **Semantic design:** specify the SCC record and reverse index, conservative D/A/U rule, newly positive-domain closure, linked properness and complete reward contract. 3. **Shadow prototype only after selection:** initially support globally routed, compatible checkpoint/offer-free entries and existing complete acyclic mandatory macros. Refuse unsupported interior cycles and unpriced-resource reuse. Qualify exact dispositions against F on the bounded controls.
4. **All-in cost decision:** measure F versus I on identical complete candidates; add no root-search budget. If useful, integrate with the separately qualified artifact owner, interruption/resource contracts and final graph gate. Otherwise retain the scoped negative.
5. **Optional persistent cache:** select only after observed repeated exact units and cold net benefit; version the full payload, test invalidation/load/release costs independently and preserve the full-path fallback.
