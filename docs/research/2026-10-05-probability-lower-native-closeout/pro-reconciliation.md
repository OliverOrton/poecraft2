## Why the controller argument transfers, and its limits

**Derived mathematics.** Let the state include the actual item and every controller memory/restore/observation coordinate needed by the native law. Let bounded nonnegative `L` vanish at accepted terminal goals. Suppose every permitted primitive, at every represented physical member and history, satisfies

`L(x) <= c(x,a) + E[L(X') | x,a]`.

An adaptive controller selects a permitted action after each observation. Conditioning on that history preserves the inequality; summing through a finite stopped prefix telescopes. For a proper terminating policy, boundedness makes the residual expected potential vanish as the stopping horizon increases. Expected paid cost is consequently at least `L(x0)`. Infinite expected paid cost is not a counterexample. The optimization domain must retain the native properness requirement; an improper zero-cost loop is not a successful completion witness.

This needs no enumeration of all adaptive controllers. It does need complete primitive and auxiliary-transition coverage. A free restore that replaces a worse current item by a valuable saved item is a transition in the augmented state, not an ordinary reroll. Forgetting saved memory can increase potential without paying for it. An observed choice must be bounded under every permitted decision after the observation; averaging choices before control is available can produce a false lower. A compressed retry basin is not a physical item and does not inherit a positive member lookup merely because its visible fields match. These are reasons to keep Imprint/retry/unsupported projection guards, not reasons to suppress an already uniform covered clean-state proof.

If two independently valid native state lowers exist, their pointwise maximum remains a lower on the same native optimization problem. That does not make their maximum a feasible initial vector in every more optimistic auxiliary model. Final-vector checking against the actual model is still necessary. An action-specific `c + continuation` floor is a lower on that action's Q value; it becomes a state minimum only after all permitted alternatives are covered. W2 concerns exactly that last distinction.
