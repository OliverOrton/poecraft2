## 4. Chosen mathematical construction

### 4.1 Model and theorem

Let z be the complete native semantic history/state, including control/restore
memory whenever relevant. Let D contain eligible physical item domains under
the **original** final goal and caller scope. Let phi map native members in D
to finitely many coordinates

    (rarity, current goal mask, prefix count, suffix count,
     selected fracture/fresh region, crafted goal mask,
     crafted prefix/suffix junk, selected filter identity).

The initial selected scope is at most five fixed-side, distinct-affix goals,
clean final success, supported live ordinary/Eldritch items, no restore memory,
no unsupported influence/veil/companion carrier. This is descriptor- and
proof-driven, not a Conquest dispatch. Unsupported requests retain zero.
Use the coupled fresh/fracture frame as construction geometry, not as a mandate
that the real policy acquire the selected fracture.

For every source coordinate q and every permitted primitive occurrence u in U,
supply one of:

- a native-containing probability/choice relation;
- proof of inapplicability for every member in the source preimage;
- an independent scalar first-exit floor valid for all applicable members.

For a relation, partition every native outcome into events i, with complete
positive mass. Let P(q,u) contain the probability vector induced by **each**
native member and relevant history. Every internal event has a set C_i of
possible successor coordinates containing the native projection. Every genuine
outside event has an independent lower b_i valid for every corresponding
original continuation member; initially zero. Observed selectors are minimized
where they really occur. Costs use a nonnegative native-guaranteed paid floor
c_minus(q,u).

For x in a bounded nonnegative box, define event values

    m_i(x) = min { x(t): t in C_i }         for internal outcomes,
             b_i                          for outside outcomes.

Use the corresponding observed-choice minimum inside each event when needed.
Require every simultaneous inequality

    x(q) <= c_minus(q,u) + inf[p in P(q,u)] sum_i p_i m_i(x),       (1)

or x(q)<=ell(q,u) for a covered scalar floor. Every true native goal maps to
a zero coordinate; auxiliary extra terminals may be deliberately favorable.

**Theorem (conditional).** Suppose:

1. phi and every member preimage, native outcome and choice are covered;
2. all allowed static/generated controllers flatten to U occurrences and
   legitimate zero-cost control updates with covered semantic states, or stop
   at the first independently bounded exit;
3. the original nonnegative paid reward is at least the modeled reward on every
   occurrence; coefficients/envelopes and b are independently valid in this
   same source/goal/scope/price generation;
4. x is bounded, nonnegative and simultaneously accepted against the complete
   current-value native minima;
5. the compared native continuation is proper with finite expected paid cost.

Then L(z)=x(phi(z)) lower-bounds every such original native continuation from
z in D, and hence the proper-policy optimum on D. Its uniform class value is
the infimum over the entire class preimage, with unsupported members
contributing their independently valid fallback, including zero.

**Proof sketch.** For each native history in D, its induced event distribution
is in P; its successor potential is at least the event minimum. Equation (1)
therefore implies the native one-step lower inequality after conditioning on
that history. Apply it repeatedly until the first goal, genuine outside exit,
scalar stop or n primitive/control steps. At a genuine exit invoke the
independent original-scope continuation lower once; do not try to prove a
Bellman inequality for b outside D. The remaining internal potential is bounded
by B times the probability of not having stopped. This tends to zero under
properness. Nonnegative partial paid sums converge monotonically. Taking the
infimum over all permitted proper policies proves the result.

This is a scoped application of CLM-0006/0007/0008/0012/0014, not a new exact
MDP solver or a convergence theorem. Bounded potentials and almost-sure stopping
suffice for the lower argument; use the stronger finite-policy lifetime/cost
obligations of the native upper checker when certifying an executable policy.
Primary SSP literature distinguishes optimal values over proper policies from
unrestricted fixed points under weak assumptions. Do not import a uniqueness
or convergence theorem without its hypotheses. [M1, M2, S12]

### 4.2 Why the probability part has real strength potential

D, synthetic: one paid action costs 1, succeeds with probability at most 1/100,
and otherwise returns to the same fully modeled state. If it is the complete
scope and its failure retains current progress, (1) permits x=100:

    x <= 1 + (99/100)x.

A support-union action that grants success immediately has ceiling 1.
Probability is therefore meaningful when it preserves the failure continuation.

Add an allowed paid outside finish of cost 7 and zero boundary: x<=7.
The same probability-aware model now has ceiling 7. This second toy is why
a success probability improvement alone is not the economic gate.

D, synthetic destructive region: state r can finish for 10, or pay 2 then
reach goal or q equally. At q, pay 1 and either return to q or go destructively
outside to d equally. With independently valid b(d), the relaxed fixed retry
tail is 2+b(d) and the r deviation costs 3+b(d)/2. Reaching threshold 10
requires b(d)>=14. A better numeric algorithm cannot repair b(d)=4.
If a legal q finish costs 1, the alleged ten lower fails by missing scope.
This illustrates why the chosen dimensions should retain destructive cost, and
why weak paid outside exits must be found before iteration. [S12]

### 4.3 Probability envelope and coefficient obligations

Reuse the existing native integer weighted-pool evidence and conditional
exclusion bounds. For satisfying weight N, other weight B and conservative
other-weight deletion budget D, the producer bounds a conditional hit by

    N / (N + max(0,B-D)),

with the actual integer total, empty-pool cases and directed arithmetic handled.
Never union hypothetical goal members and subtract their target weight from
the denominator. Every blocker/history and pool-changing prior action matters.

For complete exact-mask events, safe upper capacities may use minimum marginal
caps. Products require uniform conditional-history bounds and complete
distinct-position assignments. Retained fracture occupancy, forced/guaranteed
draws, opposite-side blockers, side limits and multi-slot overlap must be
handled. A single modifier satisfying two goals defeats injective assignments.
The chosen retention component refuses that scope initially rather than
silently treating two bits as two removable affixes.

For box constraints p_i<=u_i and sum p_i=1, compute the minimum by sorting current
event values and filling cheapest events to capacity. This is a continuous
native-containing envelope: rounding capacities upward weakens the lower; it
does not pretend the native law is an exact dyadic law. Aggregate subset
constraints would require a different minimization proof.

The actual implementation uses mass 2^24 and final-value event re-minimization.
Do not cache a selected allocation as a native kernel. With capacities
(0.6,0.6) and values (0,10), minimum 4 uses (0.6,0.4).
After values swap, the minimum remains 4 but the old allocation gives 6.
Accepting x=5 using that stale witness is unsound.

A numerical proposal is untrusted until the unchanged raw-inequality exact-dot
checker, coefficient/provenance contract and complete native event minimization
accept it. Native laws and envelope arithmetic are distinct layers. No
renormalization, tiny-probability cutoff, epsilon relaxation, old vector forced
as a lower constraint or changed acceptance tolerance is proposed. Equal-cost
shortcuts reactivate; eager supported relations remove computational placeholders
but do not remove semantic exits. [S7, S8, S12]

Thus a new side controller alone need not add a controller dimension if every
primitive and intermediate physical member is covered. Zero-cost control

the request until complete memory/control semantics exist.

### 4.5 Goal and domain counterexamples

- **Clean versus coverage.** A covered dirty item is a true goal under
  allow-extra semantics; a positive clean occupancy lower there is false.
  Initial chosen issuer refuses coverage. A later coverage-compatible component
  must set every newly true goal to zero and recheck all predecessors.
- **Any-k.** Complement lookup acquisition[full xor mask] can charge unnecessary
  slots. Use min acquisition[R] over masks with popcount(mask union R)>=k,
  or construct the correct current-progress goal predicate directly.
  The initial disjoint all-required scope does not certify any-k production.
- **One affix hits two goals.** Uniform loss arithmetic cannot count two
  independent removable occurrences. Refuse or use richer complete categories.
- **Smaller clean target.** A junk-free one-goal request is not automatically
  a relaxation of a junk-free two-goal request: the second requested modifier
  becomes junk under the smaller terminal predicate. Keep the full final goal.
- **Filter erased.** Unfiltered completion lower 100 can exceed filtered optimum
  2. Adding a chosen cleanup cost 2 yields one policy cost, not a lower on all
  allowed policies. Retain native filter identity and occupancy or stop.
- **Member broadcasting.** A class containing members with optima 2 and 100
  cannot receive lower 100 from its most convenient representative.
  Intersect complete member masks with all observed flags and identities.
- **Missing generated member.** Ten-cost completion with an omitted one-cost
  programme is not a lower. Count equality, sample admission and a completed
  finite generated prefix are insufficient.
- **Cost reuse.** A certificate at a ten-cost exit cannot survive a new one-cost
  price generation by relabeling its identity. Reprepare/recheck or refuse.
- **Additive double counting.** Two five-cost subgoals may share one five-cost
  operation. Combine compatible independent components by maximum, not sum,
  absent a complete nonnegative primitive cost partition.
